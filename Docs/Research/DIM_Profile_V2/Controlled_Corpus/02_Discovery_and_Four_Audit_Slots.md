## 1. What we learned and decided

There are credible public sources beyond marketing descriptions: Max-specific transmission examples, Raspberry Shake data-service documentation, METER device/readings examples, Campbell status examples, and Dragino configuration and payload documentation. Their availability is not equivalent to obtaining a verified installed System's records. In particular, a public interface example and authenticated access to an actual device are different evidence. The source register below records the documents actually inspected and their limits.

**Audit these four, in order:** **A — AirGradient Open Air Max; B — Raspberry Shake & Boom; C — Dragino S31-LB; D — Campbell Scientific CR1000X with ClimaVue 50.** Slot C replaces the initial ZL6 suggestion. METER ZL6 with a documented sensor arrangement is a credible reserve, not a failed or inaccessible donor; public ZENTRA examples were found. The replacement is a bounded research-selection judgment: retain one rich programmable logger case and add a simpler wireless node with explicit configuration/status/measurement messages rather than spend two of four audits on weather-logger arrangements.

These are candidates for investigation, **not four passing systems or the final two-to-three-system set**. The eight-case screen is not a comprehensive market survey. No equipment performance or military equivalence is inferred from vendor claims.

## 2. Discovery method and evidence ceiling

The screen included the four starting suggestions, two closer unattended ground sensing (UGS) comparators explicitly anticipated by the plan, and two additional open/documented alternatives. Searches combined product names with manual, configuration, payload, API, status and sample-data terms. Manufacturer/developer pages and documentation controlled the findings; public repository documentation was read where relevant. Broader results were used only to locate primary sources, not to substitute third-party claims for those sources.

For every candidate, the screen considered the same four questions: **is there a defensible equipment boundary; is whole-System documentation visible; are native records/examples documented or actually visible; and what applicability/access gaps remain?** The table gives provisional answers, not G1–G4 verdicts. The six evidence-depth scores and PASS/CONDITIONAL/FAIL belong to the later individual audits.

**Evidence actually inspected:** public product/reference pages, technical documentation, and original examples embedded in documentation. No live device was contacted, authenticated account used, waveform/audio archive downloaded, packet decoder executed, or complete source packet frozen. A source that documents a download service is a retrieval lead, not a successfully retrieved device record. No new item is asserted as **REAL_RECORDED** on the basis of this screen.

The Step 01 labels remain unchanged. Product/technical descriptions are **PRIMARY_DESIGN_OR_SPEC**; explicit vendor examples are **PRIMARY_PUBLISHED_EXAMPLE**. Unchecked firmware applicability, device joins and operational records remain **UNAVAILABLE_OR_UNVERIFIED**. Labels apply to claims, not automatically to an entire website. Documentary examples containing comments or omissions have not been established as directly parseable JSON.

## 4. Why these four — the research tradeoffs

**AirGradient is retained because its own Max firmware documentation exposes useful native examples.** That is a stronger starting point than relying on a dashboard or borrowing a payload from AirGradient ONE. Its power and reporting documentation can challenge whether a future common Details view distinguishes design capability, selected transmission mode, battery voltage and delayed/cached reporting. The audit must establish which of those statements actually apply to the chosen variant/revision; this is not yet a validated interpretation. [AG1–AG4]

**RS&BOOM is retained for the seismic/infrasound connection and a documented route to instrument metadata and waveform history.** It is not selected because every aspect of health or configuration is already public. Its audit must test exactly that limitation and obtain a small attributable record if possible. The local monitoring example is not a substitute for public telemetry from the selected instrument. [RS1–RS3]

**Dragino replaces ZL6 in slot C to vary the experiment, not to declare METER technically inferior.** The published S31-LB/S31B/LS manual offers a compact route from identifiable equipment to configuration commands and status/measurement message definitions. We deliberately choose the external-probe, primary-battery S31-LB case and leave other variants out. This is an analyst selection judgment based on [DR1], not a claim that all its documentation is correct or that its gate verdict is known.

**Campbell is retained as the modular programmable case, with its boundary narrowed to a named sensor pairing.** Its public diagnostic example offers a richer counterpoint to a simple status field. Nevertheless, the program, complete reference arrangement and applicability of the generic status example still require examination. “CR1000X + ClimaVue 50” names an audit target; it does not create an installed station or authorize invented power/enclosure details. [CA1–CA3]

The choice limits duplicated weather-logger coverage while preserving different manufacturers, modalities and structures. The price is postponing METER's useful cloud/device/port model and AudioMoth's offline recording model. Both are explicit reserves; neither is added as a fifth audit. Surveyor and RANGER remain important use-case comparators, but similar military purpose does not compensate for missing public native examples.
