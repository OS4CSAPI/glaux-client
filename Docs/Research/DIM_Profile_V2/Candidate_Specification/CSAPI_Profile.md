### Why one binding cannot represent every type of relationship

**The target that CSAPI describes is specific to each resource:** a `System` means an *individual system*, a `Procedure` can mean a product datasheet/type or a process method, and a `Deployment` describes an actual or proposed assignment of Systems in a time/space/purpose context. A **reference arrangement** of products can be described by appropriate type/method documentation **without inventing a separate installed System**. CSAPI Part 1 [§9.1, notes 1–3](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-001/23-001.html) explicitly differentiates instances and types, and [§13.1–13.2 / Table 16](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-001/23-001.html) explicitly permits sensor datasheets as Procedures. **A product datasheet and an actual observing method both use the general Procedure resource family but are not automatically the same Procedure or `ObservingProcedure`.**

Part 1 [§10.2](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-001/23-001.html) distinguishes **nested Subsystem composition** for integral parts from **aggregation** (detachable/reassignable Systems) through Deployments. SensorML 3.0 [§8.3–8.6](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-000/23-000.html) further distinguishes atomic/aggregate physical processes and data-flow connections, not an arbitrary physical child per output field. **A merely cabled or supplied external probe is not necessarily permanent composition**, and a source explaining the existence of a cable does not itself prove a particular installed Deployment.

### Open standards/encoding choices that Step 26 must not conceal

1. **Date-only operator history versus `Deployment.validTime`**: The original Part 1 `Deployment` property is required, with an actual deployment interval. The directly inspected companion GeoJSON `deployment.json` requires `validTime`; `common/timePeriod.json` uses **two `TimeInstant` or `now` endpoints**; `timeInstant.json` requires date-time format. Original Bennett use history has **calendar-day assertions only** and an as-of-2024 statement. The model can retain those as source evidence but an exact endpoint series is **not proven**. An explicit uncertain-window/precision encoding or a source-linked history extension, if adopted in the future, requires its own scoped semantics and validation. No fake midnight/current `now`.
2. **Composition versus changing aggregation**: Part 1 §10.2 intentionally uses **nested Subsystems for Composition** and **`Deployment` for Aggregation**, including a weather station with interchangeable sensors as an original example. A named, replaceable EE181 with two separately attested use periods is **not safely expressed as permanent nested composition**. Actual subtype relationships for a product design also depend on what the manufacturer means by its assembly; an arbitrary CSAPI `System` component tree cannot be inferred from packet-field counts.
3. **Source design versus real instance**: Part 1 §9.1 makes the distinction explicit. A product-only Procedure can describe a design, but it does not license creating a corresponding fielded System. The station is a source-identified real referent; its exact `systemType` and Host/platform role need a documented project decision, not simply a similarity in label.
4. **Platform vs feature target scope**: Part 1 §11.2 Table 11 conceptually permits **any Feature** as platform; the §19.1 GeoJSON mapping Table 43 explicitly points `platform@link` to a **System**; SensorML Table 52 does likewise. For Bennett a source-attested station-as-System is a reasonable candidate platform *if classified correctly*, but do **not** claim every external non-System Feature can be mapped using exactly the same documented link without additional representation review.
5. **Identities and original issuer**: Part 1 service-level UID uniqueness and SensorML identifiers are compatible carriers, not original manufacturer URI issuance. The actual station code and two physical serials are **source facts**, not preexisting CSAPI URNs. A future project UID registry needs traceability to the original nameplate/operator authority and no accidental merger of local address, serial, API ID or service gateway ID.
6. **No hidden Step 27–29 commitments**: Physical-source links may point to the actual sensing probe while a later reporting DataStream could belong to the **station/logger**; that is a *separate producing-System decision*. No observed property, channel schema, observation time, quality, state/command or original resultTime binding is decided by the presence of a `System`, `Procedure` or `Deployment` in this Step 26 proposal.

## Product model or individual unit?

**A datasheet answers “What kind of equipment is this?” A unit record answers “Which one?” An installation record answers “Where or on what was that unit installed, and when?”** These are separate claims. The [equipment identity table](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/Donor_Terms_Side_by_Side.md#equipment-identity) maps nameplate fields; this section specifies what a producer can publish from them. It applies the existing candidate DIM distinctions between [an individual System](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/README.md#individual-system) and [a model or kind](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/README.md#kind-model). No new resource class is needed.

| What the source establishes | What to publish under this candidate Profile | What a client can show |
|---|---|---|
| **A product model only.** A manufacturer identifies the model and describes its design. | Publish a datasheet `Procedure` with its own UID. Put shared specifications there. For a sensor datasheet, Part 1 §13.2.2 Table 16 lists `sosa:Sensor` as a procedure type. A `Procedure` is non-spatial (§13.2.3). Do not create a `System` merely to show the datasheet. | **Product model.** Show capabilities and documented defaults; no identified unit, installed firmware, location or current reading follows. |
| **A particular physical unit.** An attributable asset register, nameplate inspection or device response identifies one item in a known identifier scheme. | Publish a `System` with its own UID. Use an existing suitable unit URI when it identifies that same item; otherwise assign one and retain the source identifier, its kind and its issuing authority/scope. A manufacturer serial is useful but not required. Link `System → systemKind → Procedure` when the applicable datasheet is identified. | **Individual unit.** Show what identifies it. It can be known even when its model, installation or current state is unknown. Two distinct units of the same model can refer to the same datasheet. |
| **An installation of that unit.** A dated installation, removal or assignment record identifies the unit and its site or host. | Represent the evidenced association using `Deployment` and `deployedSystems`, with the host/platform and time extent as supported (Part 1 §11). Keep historical, planned and actual placement distinct; do not infer present installation from an old record. | **Unit installed at site/on host for the evidenced time.** A spare in inventory is not a deployed unit; a site name alone does not identify its current instrument. |

**How to encode the unit–model link.** In GeoJSON the unit UID is `properties.uid`; the link is `properties["systemKind@link"].href`. In SensorML JSON the corresponding members are `uniqueId` and `typeOf.href`. Resolve the link to the *datasheet Procedure*, not a second System or an observing method. The API's local `id` locates a resource within the service; it is not the manufacturer's serial or a cross-service match key. Both a sensor System and a sensor-datasheet Procedure can carry the sensor type tag, so use the resource's CSAPI role and canonical collection, not the tag or the string `PhysicalSystem` alone. Keep multiple or ambiguous source classifications as such rather than picking an unsupported datasheet link. See [Part 1 §§8.4–8.5, 9.1–9.2, 13.2, 19.1.4–5 and 19.2.4–5](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-001/23-001.html) and [SensorML 3.0 §§8.2.2.3 and 8.2.9.3](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-000/23-000.html).

**Where the design comes from.** These are correspondences for this use, not declarations that all classes in a row are equivalent.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Its `ObservingProcedure` describes the method: it is not a blanket synonym for a CSAPI product datasheet ([preserved original](../Primary_Sources/OGC_20-082r4_OMS_3.0.0.md), §§8.5.2, 8.6.2).

**Acceptance decision:** Never create or merge individual Systems solely because manufacturer and model match. These are worked documentation checks, not a live device or conformance test.

## Components and channels

[Donor terms side by side](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/Donor_Terms_Side_by_Side.md#components-and-channels) · [What the DIM means](DIM.md#components-and-channels) · [Worked SensorML JSON](examples/s31-lb-design.sml.json)

**Proposed rule:** A producer must describe evidenced physical parts separately from outputs and result fields. A client must not count channels, data streams or result fields as physical equipment. When a description identifies the origin of an output, retain that association even if another component exposes the output to the network.

| Information to exchange | Proposed binding | What it must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| **A product design with a sensing part** | Use a CSAPI `Procedure` of sensor-datasheet type. In its `application/sml+json` representation use `PhysicalSystem.components` for this aggregate design, with a named `PhysicalComponent` for the described probe. A project-issued design URI identifies the description, not a deployed unit. | No installation, serial number, deployment time or operational observation follows from the datasheet. No position in a Procedure. |
| **A component's result exposed by the reporting system** | Describe the component output and the outward system output in SensorML `outputs`; when the source supports the flow, connect them with `connections[].source` and `.destination`. For a grouped output, use a named SWE Common `DataRecord`; put each scalar measurement in a `Quantity` field with its own name, property definition and unit. | A record with two fields is not two probes. This describes logical data flow, not the raw on-wire probe protocol or a proved event. |
| **An identified installed system and its operating data** | Link the unit to its datasheet using `systemKind` (`typeOf` in SensorML). When publishing data, a `DataStream` describes one output of one producing System, with a common schema across that stream. For the grouped-output choice, the JSON observation-schema resource uses `obsFormat: application/json` and the two-field `DataRecord` as `resultSchema`; values in `Observation.result` follow it. Separate scalar outputs may instead justify separate streams, with no change to physical inventory. | A data stream is not itself a channel address or a probe. Do not create an individual unit from a model name, an IEEE address or a published example. Part 2 §9.2.2 says the server derives `observedProperties` and related summaries from linked observations, with nulls when none are linked; do not use them to advertise a mere design capability. |
| **Actual subsystem membership or source-specific interface facts** | In the CSAPI Subsystems class, use nested System resources for composition and a `Deployment` for changing aggregation (Part 1 §§10.2–10.5). A current deployed subsystem can also appear in the subsystem listing. Preserve the original module/address context, functional-realization and interface-visibility facts in source mappings; a lossless common wire binding for those extra facts is **not specified here**. | Inline design components do not substitute for required instance endpoints. Cable length does not choose between composition and deployment. Never silently discard an unmapped channel address and declare a round trip lossless. |

**Candidate exchange rules.** Adopt the following conventions for the [four DIM relationships](DIM.md#which-setting-applies). They are project choices, not new OGC requirements. The [donor table](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/Donor_Terms_Side_by_Side.md#configuration) records where their meanings come from.

| What the producer knows | Where the information goes | What the client may conclude |
|---|---|---|
| **Model supports a setting; manufacturer declares a default** | On the CSAPI datasheet `Procedure`, declare a named SensorML `parameters` entry with meaning and unit. Keep its value unset. Put the published default in a separate `characteristics` group whose `definition` means *documented equipment defaults*; use the same parameter definition URI for its value. Cite the applicable source. Only add constraints explicitly supported by it. | The model supports the setting and has that documented default. An individual that inherits the group inherits a statement about the default, not a report. |
| **An actual write request or acknowledgement exists** | Where Commands are implemented, put the requested value in `Command.parameters` per the `ControlStream` schema. Keep any linked `CommandStatus`, its status code, times and any result separate. Do not generate these resources for a manual example. | “Requested” or “accepted” does not mean “applied.” `COMPLETED` describes a successful operation; use it as as-applied evidence only when the documented operation guarantees the effective value for this target and time, not as perpetual readback. |
| **An actual readback or authoritative effective configuration exists** | For a read implemented as a command, associate the returned `CommandResult.data` with the read request via `CommandStatus.result`; check the stream's `resultSchema` and correct unit. An attributable status output can instead use a `DataStream` and `Observation` when the source actually supports an observation of that property. For a documented, time-scoped instance snapshot, bind the unit's `typeOf` and use `configuration.setValues` for an inherited parameter, or `setModes` for an evidenced mode; retain the applicable source record and validity, not just the bare number. | “Unit U reported value V at T.” Call it current only for a time its evidence covers; receiving a cached reply now does not make it effective now. | **No applicable instance evidence** | Omit the effective value for that parameter. Keep model metadata, illustrative values and any actual request history in their own roles. Do not manufacture a System, status report, Observation, zero, or open-ended validity interval. | “Current setting: unknown.” Show an attributable older value only as last reported, with its time when known. |

### Did the node switch modes?

**Report the status of the task and the mode of the node separately.** A SAPIENT `TaskAck` with `task_status = ACCEPTED` means the node agreed to carry out the identified task. It does not say the node has entered the requested mode. A `StatusReport.mode` from that node is direct evidence of the mode it reports at the time of the message content. These are applications of the existing [DIM configuration relationships](DIM.md#which-setting-applies) and [donor status crosswalk](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/Donor_Terms_Side_by_Side.md#task-responses), not new equipment classes.

| What arrived from SAPIENT | Where it belongs in the proposed Profile | What a client may say |
|---|---|---|
| Registration lists a named mode; a `Task` requests it via `command.mode_change`. | Preserve the node-scoped declaration. Map the actual task and its requested mode to `Command.parameters` under the applicable control-stream schema. | “Supported” and “requested,” not “active.” A registered default is not a status report. |
| Matching `TaskAck.task_id` has `task_status = ACCEPTED`. | Link `CommandStatus.statusCode = ACCEPTED` to that command, with a supported `reportTime`. Do not write the target mode into the node’s reported configuration. | “Mode change accepted.” The earlier reported mode and its time remain available; absent any report, the mode is unknown. |
| A later `TaskAck` reports `COMPLETED` or `FAILED` for that same task. | Record the stated task outcome. For an exact, applicable mode-change operation, completion can support an **as-applied inference**; label it as inferred from task completion, not direct readback. CSAPI requires actual execution time for `COMPLETED`: retain the native acknowledgement when that time is unavailable rather than inventing it. A failure does not tell us which mode is active. | “Node reports that the change completed” or “Node reports that the change failed.” Neither alone proves the mode still applies now. |
| A `StatusReport` from the target node names its mode. | Update that node’s last-reported mode and source time. Preserve the separate task outcome. Where the report represents an actual observation of mode, use the configuration reporting binding above, not `CommandStatus` for the mode itself. | “Node reported *follow* at 10:02.” This can be true even when no completion acknowledgement was received. A matching name and nearby time do not prove a particular task caused the state. |

**Worked sequence — illustrative only, not captured traffic.** Assume the node registered the names `default` and `follow`. At 09:59 it reports `default`; at 10:00 it is asked to switch to `follow`; at 10:01 it accepts. At that point the two answers are **Task: accepted. Last reported mode: default at 09:59.** At 10:02 the node reports `follow`. Now the answers are **Task: accepted (no completion report received). Last reported mode: follow at 10:02.** The reports establish different modes at two times, not the exact instant of transition or that the node is ready at full performance.

**Implementation checks.** Correlate the request target with the responding node and the acknowledgement with its `task_id`. Retain status `report_id`, sender and content timestamp. `active_task_id`, when supplied, identifies an executing task; it is not a completion acknowledgement. An older report arriving late must not replace a newer last-reported value; a report from another node cannot update this node. Temporary modes can revert, and the registered `settle_time` describes a transition characteristic, not a live readiness result. For a single-mode node, SAPIENT says to ignore `mode_change`; do not infer a transition or fabricate an acknowledgement code.

**Candidate rule for incomplete input:** keep a verifiably linked reply even when its outcome is missing. Flag that the printed example lacks a field [normative Table 86](https://www.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/en/insights-and-media/insights/brochures/bsi-flex-335.pdf#page=84) marks mandatory. Missing in the source is not an explicitly transmitted `UNSPECIFIED`, `ACCEPTED`, or `PENDING`. In particular, absence of evidence of a decision is not evidence that a decision is pending. Do not emit a CSAPI `CommandStatus` from that reply alone: [`statusCode` is required, Part 2 §10.11, Tables 13–14](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-002/23-002.html). Preserve the native evidence for review rather than fabricating a valid-looking resource. Do not update the node's mode or timestamp from a task reply.
