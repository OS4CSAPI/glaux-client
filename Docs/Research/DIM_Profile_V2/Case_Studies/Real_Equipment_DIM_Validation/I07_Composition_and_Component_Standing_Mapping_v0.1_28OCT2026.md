## 1. The concrete result

**Smart Citizen Kit 2.3 is a real, documented modular equipment design that ordinarily combines two mating electronic boards, mounted sensing chips, a separate cabled particulate-matter sensor, and supporting power/storage equipment.** The manufacturer's step-by-step **device assembly** instructions are particularly valuable: they physically join the Data Board and Urban Board, seat the particulate module **in the same enclosure clip**, connect it by a **custom cable**, then install the battery and secure the assembly in the enclosure ([D08, manufacturer assembly steps](https://github.com/fablabbcn/smartcitizen-docs/blob/0a18bc0bd372d2bcedfca5bc5e6109f88ea8519f/docs/guides/getting-started/kit-assembly.md#L114-L149); [D02, Urban Board and PM connections](https://github.com/fablabbcn/smartcitizen-docs/blob/0a18bc0bd372d2bcedfca5bc5e6109f88ea8519f/docs/hardware/boards/urban-board.md#L31-L45); Smart Citizen 2026).

This makes the prior Pass 4 research useful in a way a generic hierarchy could not: **physical attachment, replacement capability, independent equipment standing, and digital/operational relationships are different questions**. A cable and connector do **not** automatically make the PM sensor a temporary *external* payload; the source actually places it as part of the typical assembled Kit. Conversely, a soldered multichannel sensor chip does **not** automatically have its own managed System identity, simply because its temperature, humidity or ultraviolet outputs can be distinguished.

**The first question is “How is an element related to the whole?” The second is “How independently does the original source describe it, and how much independent standing does an integration really need?”** This iteration answers these questions *for documented design relationships*. It does not pretend to reconstruct a verified fielded SCK2.3 unit.

### Three important boundaries the equipment proves useful for

**First: the PM sensor belongs in the normal assembled Kit despite being cabled.** The manufacturer instructs the assembler to put the PM module in the enclosure clip with the boards and attach the PM cable. For this source-defined assembly, treating that module as merely a networked remote sensor would misrepresent the physical source. But the same manufacturer supports alternate SEN5X model options and board replacement. **Built into one assembled design does not prove never interchangeable**, and interchangeable design does not prove repeatedly exchanged during deployment. These are different propositions requiring different evidence.

**Second: a separately describable Data Board need not be a separate sensing System.** The Data Board has a documented processor, communications, flash, power management and distinct design files; the Urban Board has separate sensor electronics and a defined physical connector. That gives us meaningful **board-level decomposition**, without evidence that either board was independently deployed, assigned a persistent equipment identity, or offered as a separately managed CSAPI System. The Data Board contributes non-sensing functions to an overarching **sensing-oriented** whole. Neither board needs to be turned into an invented V2 `DataBoardSystem` or `SensingAssembly` class.

**Third: output channels and physical devices do not have one-to-one cardinality.** The SHT31 handles both temperature and humidity; the AS7331 provides UV-A, UV-B and UV-C. One package can support several measurements. Modeling each channel as a discrete permanently installed physical instrument would overstate what the 2.3 source reports. Conversely, if a future source identifies separately managed chips or channel-specific calibration, we should preserve *that actual information* instead of forbidding finer detail ([D02, SCK2.3 channel/sensor table](https://github.com/fablabbcn/smartcitizen-docs/blob/0a18bc0bd372d2bcedfca5bc5e6109f88ea8519f/docs/hardware/boards/urban-board.md#L47-L59); [H03, mounted sensor parts](https://github.com/fablabbcn/smartcitizen-kit-2x/blob/3dbbc1061509920aca2dda2aca32abe82563500b/hardware/SCK2.3/SCK_URBAN_BOARD_2.3/SCK_URBAN_BOARD_2.3.sch#L7496-L7543); Smart Citizen 2026; Fab Lab Barcelona 2026).

## 3. What the donor standards actually contribute

**OGC API – Connected Systems Part 1, version 1.0 — integral composition versus interchangeable aggregation.** CSAPI § 10.2 defines **Composition** for a **subsystem that is integral to a parent and not usually separated except for maintenance**, even if independent physical existence is possible. Its **Aggregation** describes a subsystem that is **not permanently attached**, can change its parent over time, and is represented using **Deployment** resources. Its § 10.1/10.3 subsystems are **regular `System` resources**. Thus **SCK2.3's assembled board and PM-module relationships can be evaluated against these meanings**, but we cannot conclude from an assembly manual that each merits an independent `System` resource or that component exchanges occurred. **An operationally installed, maintenance-replaceable PM module is not automatically CSAPI Aggregation**. These CSAPI semantics also do **not** justify importing stricter UML composite-aggregation diamonds (Open Geospatial Consortium 2025a, §§ 10.1–10.3).

**OGC SensorML 3.0 — decomposition and process connections depend on description scope.** In its native model, `PhysicalComponent` describes a physical process not further divided into subprocesses at the chosen granularity, while `PhysicalSystem` provides a description **decomposed** into component processes and their connections (Open Geospatial Consortium 2025b, §§ 8.4–8.6). This is applicable to our **documented whole-Kit assembly** and its board/sensor/process relationships, particularly because I06 documented measurement and computational functions. **A SensorML process component is not automatically an independent CSAPI System or physical maintenance item**, and the choice to write one detailed SensorML process description remains separate from the DIM-level concept question.

**SOSA/SSN, precisely the 21 October 2026 Working Draft — subsystem versus hosted/deployed.** Its `sosa:hasSubSystem` allows meaningful constituent Systems, while `sosa:hosts` and `sosa:Deployment` address distinct contexts. The draft's § 8.15 integrated temperature/humidity example shows how a multichannel packaged device **can** be separately described as constituent sensing Systems **when relevant**, but not that an SHT31's two measurements compel two separately managed physical chip identities (World Wide Web Consortium and Open Geospatial Consortium 2026, §§ 5.3.5 and 8.15). **The ontology draft is not a published normative update of the 2017 ontology version depended upon by CSAPI 1.0**.

**OPC UA Part 100: Devices, version 1.05.0 — device/component detail versus topology.** A `ComponentType` can represent a Device or mechanical/software parts of a Device; a complex Device's components may be reached from a root Device representation. OPC UA also keeps its **device communication model** and **device integration host model** distinct from device composition (OPC Foundation n.d., §§ 4.1, 4.6, 4.9, 5–6). These patterns explain why an integration may need a Data Board or sensor-module description, **without** treating remote API/cloud endpoints, Wi-Fi links, physical hosting or connector interfaces as intrinsic hardware part-of relations. **No OPC UA DeviceSet or actual OPC UA instance was built for this Kit.**

**Do not equate donor vocabulary on sight.** CSAPI `Composition`, SOSA `hasSubSystem`, SensorML `PhysicalSystem.components`, and OPC UA `ComponentType` have **different formal subjects and constraints**. Likewise CSAPI `Aggregation` is not automatically an OPC modular-subdevice relation, and SensorML `attachedTo` (a physical-process spatial association) does not by itself prove permanence or System ownership. We have mapped **useful meanings and boundaries**, not asserted all four models are equivalent.

## 4. A bounded “independent standing” test on our real source

The **manufacturer's actual detail** gives a better basis for selecting future descriptions than the simple physical hierarchy:

- **Whole equipment** — product-level SCK2.3 design clearly deserves whole-Kit description, though **no individual Kit identity is authenticated**.
- **Board/module level** — the Data Board, Urban Board and PM unit have separate mechanical/electrical descriptions and different functional or option profiles. They are **meaningful candidates for nested descriptive components**; only the source-specific requirements of an actual integration can justify exposing any as independent System resources.
- **Integrated-chip level** — specific chip models and shared sensing channels are documented; their **model names are not serial numbers**, and their physical presence does not automatically demand independent management.
- **Support and context level** — battery, internal memory, removable SD card, enclosure, outdoor mounting, optional auxiliary payloads, firmware, interfaces and cloud processing all matter to the **whole System** goal, but **they are not the same relationship** and should not all be converted into sensing subsystems.

**No positive claim of stand-alone subsystem identity has been demonstrated.** To justify it in a later example, we would need the relevant source to establish such things as independent equipment identifiers, replaceability history, separately addressable outputs/commands, its own configuration/calibration, a distinct deployment/host relation or another real operational need—and preserve which is *evidence* versus *design selection*. This is **a project evaluation heuristic**, not a new conformance checklist or rule every subsystem must satisfy.

## 5. I07 outcome, limitations, and what I08 will test

**SUPPORTED at product/design level:** A real modular physical whole with separate board assemblies; board-to-board physical/electrical connection; **onboard mounted sensor ICs**; a separately cabled but normal **in-enclosure PM module**; battery and storage; optional expansion and mounting contexts; and distinct communications/software beyond the housing. Each row above cites source material and links to I03 F###. I04's retraction of false sensor part-number conflicts and its design-backed flash correction remain controlling.

**PARTIAL:** Distinguishing which elements are *integral in the standard assembly* versus *interchangeable over time* is supported by product documentation, but **no actual exchange history or persistent module identity** has been observed. **NOT TESTED:** the real fielded assembly, serial/installed parts, how a deployed unit and its subparts persist through replacement, or whether any of those appear as separate CSAPI `System` or `Deployment` resources. This preserves **Gate A's CONDITIONAL verdict** from I04 rather than presenting the design as a verified individual fielded sensing system.

**The useful result:** Our fourth candidate finding survives contact with a real manufacturer's assembly instructions **without forcing every sensor channel into a subsystem**, and without falsely classifying a cable-connected PM module as merely external. That is a meaningful interoperability safeguard.
