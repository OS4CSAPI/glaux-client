## 1. What we learned by actually applying the research

**The Smart Citizen Kit 2.3 (SCK2.3) documentation gives us an identifiable *design/generation and technical specification*, but it does not give us the individual equipment item we would need to fully instantiate and test an individual System description.** The manufacturer's Kit pages distinguish SCK2.1, SCK2.2 and SCK2.3, including their interchangeable boards, version-specific sensors and different particulate-matter options (Smart Citizen 2026, [D01, lines 59–123](https://github.com/fablabbcn/smartcitizen-docs/blob/0a18bc0bd372d2bcedfca5bc5e6109f88ea8519f/docs/hardware/kit/index.md#L59-L123); Fab Lab Barcelona 2026a, [H01, lines 30–39](https://github.com/fablabbcn/smartcitizen-kit-2x/blob/3dbbc1061509920aca2dda2aca32abe82563500b/hardware/README.md#L30-L39)). This is reusable information about *what a Kit of that kind is designed to be*, not a serial-specific profile of one Kit.

**The existing OGC API – Connected Systems (CSAPI) distinction fits this situation directly at the level of meaning:** a `System` resource describes an **individual** System; its optional `systemKind` association points to a `Procedure` resource used to describe the *kind of System*, including a product datasheet (Open Geospatial Consortium 2025a, §§ 9.1–9.2.2, 13.1). A different association, `procedures`, describes methodologies the System can implement; **it is not the kind link**. The supported role is “an individual System **has a description of its kind**,” not a newly invented `ProductModel` class or a rule that a manufacturer product name is automatically a globally identified Procedure.

**Our actual case-study outcome is therefore deliberately asymmetric:** the **kind/model side has manufacturer evidence**; the **individual System side is still unpopulated**. Four candidate platform identifiers, `18486–18489`, **appear in unexecuted official example code**; we have not established the physical hardware they refer to, their registration records, or any actual readings (Fab Lab Barcelona 2026b, [R01, lines 159–170](https://github.com/fablabbcn/smartcitizen-data/blob/4f024f4d42cb40c2cd515062ee7e4815bd2798eb/examples/notebooks/learning/01_basic_data_exploration.ipynb#L159-L170); [I04, FLAG-05](I04_Independent_Evidence_Audit_and_Gate_A_v0.1_27OCT2026.md#flag-05--platform-record-candidates-versus-actual-data)). Neither `SCK2.3` nor a registration-page number can substitute for proven individual equipment identity.

This is a **useful partial validation** of Passes 1–2: our first test prevents us from misclassifying a general equipment specification as an individual fielded sensor. It **does not yet verify** the individual-to-kind link for any actual Kit.

## 3. Donor-to-case correspondence — *meaning first*, not class equivalence

**OGC CSAPI Part 1 (published v1.0)** — **direct target semantics**. Its `System` denotes an individual System. A fielded SCK2.3 whose **primary activity is sensing** and whose **realization is hardware equipment** would fit the standard's `systemType = Sensor` and `assetType = Equipment` framing (Open Geospatial Consortium 2025a, § 9.2.2). Its `uniqueIdentifier` is a required **individual System** resource attribute, distinct from a resource URL; we have **no source-validated value to use here**. `systemKind` is the **optional association** to a kind/datasheet **`Procedure` resource**; `procedures` independently relates to Procedures the System can implement. **The published manufacturer Kit documentation has not been shown to exist as an actual CSAPI Procedure resource, and no actual SCK System resource exists in this test.** So the semantic mapping is supported; any concrete CSAPI binding instance is **unimplemented and unverified** (Open Geospatial Consortium 2025a, §§ 8.5, 9.2.2, 13.1).

**OGC SensorML 3.0 (published)** — **reusable general-process versus particular configured-process pattern**. Its `typeOf` and `configuration` concepts support a manufacturer's general sensor model description and a later individual instance's chosen settings. The Kit's published supported variations give a realistic reason to distinguish those two kinds of information. But **we do not have a manufacturer-authored SensorML description for SCK2.3**, much less a real Kit's instance configuration, so this is **a candidate representation pattern**, not proof the source is encoded that way (Open Geospatial Consortium 2025b, § 8.2.9.3).

**ETSI SAREF 4.1.1** — **close semantic alignment, different ontology**. `saref:DeviceKind` is a reusable archetype that can describe models in catalogues; `saref:Device` denotes a particular tangible device, and `saref:hasDeviceKind` links it to its kind. SCK2.3's model-level and board-specific information could supply source meaning for such a kind **if an actual mapping were authored**. Neither a SAREF Device individual nor a formal DeviceKind individual was supplied in the Smart Citizen source. **Do not assert `saref:DeviceKind` = CSAPI's Procedure carrier** (European Telecommunications Standards Institute 2024, “Device kinds and devices”).

**Industrial Digital Twin Association AAS 3.2** — **independent type/instance corroboration**. An `AssetKind = Type` can describe a product/type, whereas an `AssetKind = Instance` describes an individually realized asset; the official example relates one temperature-sensor type to multiple physical units. Our case resembles that *distinction*, but neither an AAS Shell nor a globally identified SCK2.3 Type Asset is present in the frozen manufacturer source. **AAS `AssetInformation.assetType` refers to the asset's *type***, whereas **CSAPI `assetType = Equipment` classifies its physical realization**—a particularly hazardous same-name/different-meaning trap (Industrial Digital Twin Association 2026, “Types and Instances” and “Asset Information Attributes”; Open Geospatial Consortium 2025a, § 9.2.2).

**OPC UA Devices 1.05.0** — **partial nameplate analogy**. The OPC UA `IVendorNameplateType` distinguishes `Manufacturer`, `Model`, `ProductCode`, hardware/software revisions and individual `SerialNumber`/`ProductInstanceUri`. Smart Citizen gives us product-generation and engineering revision evidence, **not an instance's OPC UA nameplate**. Its `DeviceType` is an OPC UA information-model ObjectType, not automatically a manufacturer's SCK2.3 product designation (OPC Foundation n.d., §§ 4.5.2, 4.7).

**W3C WoT Thing Model 1.1; SOSA/SSN 21 October 2026 Working Draft** — **bounded analogies, not implementation commitments**. A WoT Thing Model captures reusable *interaction/interface patterns*, not necessarily the product specification of an environmental-monitoring Kit. The newer SOSA/SSN Working Draft (§ 8.16) recommends a class/individual distinction for kinds and instances; it is **not** published CSAPI 1.0's same normative-edition dependency. We have **no published WoT Thing Model or verified SOSA individual for this Kit**. Neither analogy proves a manufacturer model identifier nor obliges the DIM to manufacture ontology classes (World Wide Web Consortium 2023, §§ 1.2 and 9; World Wide Web Consortium and Open Geospatial Consortium 2026, § 8.16).

## 4. Does this deliver something real, or merely repeat a distinction?

**It delivers a meaningful *information-integrity test* for the intended integration**. For example, taking `SCK2.3` as a serial identifier would collapse many potentially different fielded units into one; treating `SEN50` and `SEN55` as installed in every Kit would turn optional component capabilities into asserted individual configuration; treating `18486` as a manufacturer serial would erase the platform/physical distinction; treating `SAMverNum = 0.9.11` as an observed unit firmware revision would attribute software-source state to physical equipment. We now have fact IDs and donor-specific conceptual distinctions that prevent **each of those concrete errors**.

**It has *not* yet demonstrated a positive physical-identity match** (one particular Kit with a verified model, physical identifier, installed assembly and platform representation). There is also no actual CSAPI System, sensor-kind Procedure resource, SAREF DeviceKind individual, AAS Shell or WoT Thing Model for this case. The five standards above supply **applicable meanings**, not a fictitious multi-standard deployment. Thus the outcome is **SUPPORTED on product-kind differentiation; PARTIAL on intended System-to-kind relationship; NOT TESTED on fielded-unit identity and source identity reconciliation**.

A DIM slice will ultimately need to distinguish an **individual subject** from its **reusable model description** and its source identity/representation references. But **I05 neither adopts new V2-owned names nor decides** how to model kind-version identity, how many kind descriptions may apply, how to preserve post-replacement continuity, or whether to make kind a separate DIM class. Those design decisions still depend on justified donor mappings and the evidence we obtain later. A simple two-box diagram would not demonstrate anything further here.
