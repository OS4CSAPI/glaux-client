# Whole-System DIM/Profile V2 — Working Workbook

**Step 01 baseline · 31 October 2026 · Read this first**

**Status:** Initial project/evidence baseline **verified and recorded**. No new Domain Information Model (DIM) class, OGC API – Connected Systems (CSAPI) Profile requirement, data transformation, or client behavior is adopted here. This workbook is the **cumulative, human-readable working area** for the [43-step plan](../PLAN_DIM_Profile_V2_Whole_System_Recovery_to_Delivery_v0.1_31OCT2026.md), not another independent research campaign.

## 1. What we are trying to build

Imagine opening the same Resource Details view for two very different sensing systems. One has a single cabled probe producing temperature and humidity; another has numerous internal sensing modules; another has a separate logger and weather sensor; yet another replaced a physical temperature probe halfway through its historical record.

A good client must recognize **what the equipment is, what it can do, what it actually did, how the pieces relate, and how much the source really establishes**. It must make those facts understandable without needing new, hard-coded presentation rules for each manufacturer. At the same time, it must not pretend that an advertised option is installed, a device manual is an actual unit record, a logger is necessarily the sensing element, or an old observation is today's equipment condition.

The intended outputs remain **three different things**:

- **DIM:** a defensible, *implementation-independent* set of common meanings and relationships for the whole sensing System, with appropriate specializations and explicit limits.
- **CSAPI application Profile:** a separate, testable way for producers to communicate those justified meanings through existing CSAPI, Sensor Model Language (SensorML), SWE Common, and other appropriate standard structures.
- **Glaux proof:** the **same meaning-aware reading path** interprets more than one independently documented equipment arrangement, then displays useful and correctly qualified information in the existing Resource Details UI.

**The project can be successful within a stated scope without claiming all vendors, all 182 source artifacts, or adoption as an OGC standard.** The final scope and overarching domain/chassis name remain research questions, not decisions made by this workbook.

## 4. What we have NOT proved — and must keep visible

**No common reader yet.** Existing experiments include particular rendering paths and manually authored terms. Passing a demo does not mean we can interpret another independently described System without new vendor/case code.

**No integrated, accepted whole-System DIM yet.** The first five conceptual meanings are promising but are not mandatory classes. Earlier source selection was not fully neutral; later donors may introduce, split, combine or replace those distinctions. The [corrective drift study](../DIM_Profile_V2_AI_Hallucination_and_Drift_Case_Study_v0.1_30OCT2026.md) prohibits automatically choosing power/“operational condition” or any other unearned priority.

**No comprehensive, equivalent real dataset for all cases.** A reference design is useful for testing design-time semantics but cannot establish an installed component, selected setting, current condition or actual command outcome.

**No fully solved Bennett time/quality/Profile encoding.** The [31-October observation-encoding review](../Candidate_Specification/Observation_Encoding_Decision_31OCT2026.md) documents real server limitations. The preserved 15-minute window and original NOAA QA token are additional **archival report context**, not native interval-valued `phenomenonTime` or a certified source `resultTime`. **Two distinct measurements are represented by four server Observation resources.** No physical installation geometry was inferred.

**No assumed donor equality or conformance.** The research corpus contains 182 *artifacts*, not 182 independently assessed standards. The [donor-first review](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/Donor_Contributions.md) records incomplete and uneven coverage. The original source documents govern actual definitions; a mapping asserted by this project is not a donor-standard requirement.

## 5. Step 02 — How we will judge whether meaning survives

**Status: Step 02 COMPLETE, 31 October 2026.** This is a common testing method, **not** a new DIM class, an adopted Profile rule, or an executed cross-vendor test. It answers one human question: *Can the same client accurately understand what two different equipment sources mean, or does information become lost, invented, or confused along the way?*

For example, if a manufacturer says **one external probe measures temperature and humidity**, it would be wrong for our client to show **two installed probes** simply because two outputs exist. Our rubric must identify the original fact, the intended common meaning, how we encoded it, what the client actually displayed, and **where an error first appeared**.

### 5.1 Five questions for every claim or relationship

| Question | What we must write down |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Source: What was actually established?** | Original source wording, link/edition/locator, value and units if relevant, **which subject** the fact concerns, its time and scope, and its evidence standing. Do not silently replace manufacturer terminology with ours. |
| **2. DIM: What does it mean beyond that manufacturer?** | Proposed source-neutral meaning and relationships, original donor support and exact qualifiers. State **fits / needs revision / unresolved**; do not force new material into the existing five candidate headings. |
| **3. Profile/producer: How can it be communicated?** | Relevant CSAPI/SensorML/SWE Common resource, field, link or vocabulary, with the standard’s actual authority versus our proposed binding identified. Record **actual encoded output** if produced; otherwise mark **not tested**. |
| **4. Client: What did Glaux really understand and show?** | Actual interpreted subject/relationship, visible text, source/uncertainty qualifiers and raw fields. Disclose any **vendor-name or case-ID-specific rule** instead of claiming a common interpreter. |
| **5. Fidelity: Did the meaning survive?** | Compare source against actual encoded and displayed results; assign the result below, identify the **first demonstrated loss/misstatement point**, and link the evidence. A build passing is not enough. |

**Minimum reusable test record:** case; source document/locator and native claim; evidence category; subject, time and applicability; candidate DIM meaning with rationale; proposed Profile binding; actual produced CSAPI record (or “not produced”); actual shared Glaux output (or “not run”); fidelity result; first supportable fault location; source/implementation/test links. Make one comparison per independently testable **fact or relationship**, not one per pretty UI card. Retain different representations of the *same* observation as one underlying source event, not different measurements.

### 5.2 Two separate classifications — *what kind of evidence* versus *did it work*

The project’s evidence labels from the [plan](../PLAN_DIM_Profile_V2_Whole_System_Recovery_to_Delivery_v0.1_31OCT2026.md) are attached **to individual claims**, not indiscriminately to an entire device or standard. A report can contain source-backed product-design information, project calculations and a later successful software test at the same time.

| Evidence label | What it means — and does not mean |
| --- | --- |
| `SOURCE_FACT` | Explicitly supported source information **within its actual scope**. A manufacturer’s design claim is a fact *about the design*, not a verified field installation. |
| `SOURCE_EXAMPLE` | A source-published sample/record, **not** evidence that our selected hardware actually emitted it. Preserve its publication context. |
| `DERIVED` | A reproducible calculation or join made by this project from named source inputs; for example, Bennett’s Point UTC conversion or equipment-history attribution. Do not say the CSV supplied the serial. |
| `CANDIDATE_MAPPING` | Our interpretation or proposed DIM/Profile correspondence, **not** an adopted donor or OGC requirement. |
| `VERIFIED_RUNTIME` | An actual, versioned software/server test with recorded input, output and assertion. It proves only those assertions, not source authenticity or universal interoperability. |
| `UNKNOWN` | The source did not establish the fact or its applicability. Keep it unknown; **not zero, false, absent hardware, or a current status value**. |
| `REJECTED_EQUIVALENCE` | A source-grounded bad identification explicitly ruled out; e.g., **two measured properties ≠ two physical probes**. Useful for negative tests. |

**Fidelity result** is independently assigned *after* comparing inputs and outputs:

| Result | Exact use |
| --- | --- |
| **PRESERVED** | Executed check confirms source subject, value/meaning, relationship and qualifiers remain correct. |
| **PRESERVED WITH QUALIFICATION** | Meaning survives, explicitly identified as, for example, **documented capability** or **historical report**, not installed state or current telemetry. |
| **SOURCE UNAVAILABLE / NOT ASSERTED** | No source evidence for that requested fact. Correctly saying “unknown” is **not** an integration failure. |
| **NOT TESTED** | One or more required model/producer/client stages haven’t actually been exercised; documentation or a plausible mapping is not a pass. |
| **LOST / OMITTED** | The source-established fact disappears from an inspected later stage where our defined test expected it. Cite both sides. |
| **MISREPRESENTED** | A later stage invents, changes, merges or overstates source subject, identity, state, meaning, time, unit or relationship. **Hard failure**, however attractive the page. |
| **CONFLICT / UNRESOLVED** | Source claims conflict or sound competing meanings/bindings remain unsettled. Show alternatives, not fake completion. |

**Do not compute a success percentage yet.** Counts of PASS in old research plans, records returned by a server and display cards are not a shared denominator for source-to-client meaning.

### 5.3 Diagnose the earliest supported problem, not the most visible symptom

| Where the problem begins | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| **Source/evidence gap** | Was this fact ever documented for this subject, time and product version? If no, use `UNKNOWN`, not “lost in CSAPI.” |
| **DIM meaning gap** | Does our model collapse distinct physical components, functions, channels, observer/reporting roles, settings or events? |
| **Profile/binding gap** | Is the DIM distinction clear, but the specified standards carrier, semantic definition or required link wrong, unjustified or missing? |
| **Producer/adapter loss** | Was a source-backed meaning dropped or changed while producing the CSAPI representation? |
| **API/server/format loss** | Did a correct submitted value fail the actual API round trip because of this implementation or encoding? Record endpoint and response. |
| **Shared reader/UI error** | Was the correct meaning retrieved but misinterpreted, put in the wrong view or presented as a stronger claim? |
| **Cannot localize yet** | We lack adjacent before/after artifacts. Record the needed comparison; **do not guess the cause**. |

An error may appear in Glaux but originate earlier. More than one problem may exist. The aim is to fix **the first justified cause**, not patch the screen until it looks convincing.

### 5.4 Concrete positive and negative examples — Dragino external probe

The [Dragino S31-LB source sheet, C-I01–C-I03, C-I08 and C-I10](../Controlled_Corpus/System_Data_Sheets/Dragino_S31_LB.md) documents the manufacturer’s **reference design**: one cabled external **SHT31 temperature/humidity probe** and the two supported measurement properties. The sheet explicitly has **no authenticated physical unit or current telemetry**. So the source supports a product-design fact—not a claim that any specific unit is deployed or taking a reading.

**Positive scenario (NOT YET EXECUTED):** one common set of DIM/Profile meanings communicates **one physical sensing component** with **two supported measured properties/outputs**. Without a manufacturer-specific rule, Glaux shows one *documented* probe arrangement and two *supported* outputs, clearly qualified as reference design. If this succeeds with a second independently documented, different system using the **same interpretation path**, the specific design-level relation can earn **PRESERVED WITH QUALIFICATION**. Today it is **NOT TESTED**.

**Negative scenario (would be MISREPRESENTED):** a client turns the two outputs into **two separately installed probes**, or shows a present temperature reading simply because the product manual says temperature is supported. Both would exceed the source evidence. If the produced CSAPI resource already invents the second probe, the fault starts in the **producer** (or earlier model/binding); if the representation correctly describes one probe and the client renders two, the fault is in the **reader/UI**. If the DIM *requires* one physical sensor per output, that is the **DIM's error**. This is a `REJECTED_EQUIVALENCE` test, not a newly invented standard rule.

**Existing historical counterexample (not a new test):** [Bennett’s Point](../Candidate_Specification/examples/bennetts-point-ee181.json) contains **two real historical temperature measurements** while the [current preservation experiment](https://github.com/OS4CSAPI/glaux-client/blob/work/details-view-20261023/Docs/Design/BENNETT_OBSERVATION_CONTEXT_BRIDGE.md) has four server Observation resources: two original scalar records and two richer representations. A client showing **four physical measurements** would be **MISREPRESENTED**. A client that shows all four as explicitly labeled *resources* might preserve raw source transparency, but has **not automatically proved general, source-neutral deduplication**. Prior round-trip tests cover specific data preservation, not broad interoperability.

### 5.5 Later success requires cross-case interpretation, not merely a successful endpoint

Within a bounded case, report what actually passed **at its evidence level**, and what remains unknown or untested. A source design can test *design-level meanings*; it cannot prove installed state, executed control or current condition. A server response can prove a response was returned, but not the underlying historical truth of a manufacturer/field record.

For the plan’s eventual cross-vendor proof, require at least **two meaningfully different, independently documented arrangements** processed through **one common interpretation**, without vendor- or case-ID-dependent meaning rules. The same interpretation must preserve important differences and unknowns instead of filling gaps with plausible but invented facts. Bennett’s Point is a candidate *separate historical-unit/time test*, not equivalent to all the model-level cases.

**Outcome of Step 02:** We have defined a falsifiable testing method, but **no new Profile conformance result, software test, model choice or cross-vendor PASS**. Case-source coverage and permissible evidence claims will be reconciled in Step 03.

### 13.3 What recurring patterns actually survive—and their limits

**A. There are physical arrangements and information-bearing outputs, but they do not have one universal shape.** Dragino's single probe has **two** measured properties; AirGradient has **multiple** physical devices but still more reported fields; ClimaVue is **one** externally connected sensing assembly with **fourteen** defined weather values; Bennett has **different identifiable probes at successive times** but a continuing station. **Analyst inference:** a future common representation must be able to express those distinctions **without inferring a physical object for every result field**. This does **not** prove that every constituent merits a standalone System, that every connection is a containment relation, or that the final boundary must be a particular root class.

**B. The actor that senses need not be the actor that collects, transforms, stores or publishes.** In Dragino, the **probe** and reporting **node** have different roles. AirGradient's modules and main board share an enclosure while the cloud remains external. The ClimaVue and CR1000X are separate instruments; Bennett's logged average and NOAA archival QA add still more processing/reporting roles. **Analyst inference:** physical connection, containment, observation, processing, persistence and publication cannot safely be treated as one relation merely because a common client ultimately displays the information. **Which roles belong to the eventual DIM boundary and how CSAPI DataStream ownership works remain open.**

**C. Identity and time are local to the actual referent.** The Dragino decoder's model label is **not** an authenticated Device EUI. Campbell's SDI-12 address is **not** a physical serial. AirGradient's example location ID or placeholder serial does **not** establish a monitor. At Bennett, the same **EE181 serial** can have two distinct service periods, while station identity continues through replacement; the actual CSV temperature row contains no probe ID. **Analyst inference:** a candidate whole-System description must not silently merge **model, individual, address, station, deployment period and information-record identity**. Which source-native identifiers are appropriate and when is a later standards/architecture question.

**D. Output values and their provenance can change before they reach a user.** AirGradient's **raw electrodes versus derived-looking cloud gas outputs**, Campbell's **absolute versus adjusted pressure and carried-forward wind**, and Bennett's **archived mean, quality and derived probe attribution** expose several different steps. Dragino's FPort 3 history format and the local-time rendering issue supply a distinct example of **decoder presentation versus original epoch**. **Analyst inference:** a common display must know the level of meaning supported and refrain from calling all returned data instantaneous, direct, fresh, calibrated or independently observed. This does not yet establish one shared observation schema or a valid standards encoding for every case.

**E. Supported capabilities and actual operating state are not interchangeable.** Three manufacturer design cases document commands, firmware interfaces, mode/power behavior and reporting formats **without one verified corresponding physical unit**. Bennett provides **dated maintenance and physical-use history**, but not a complete command/acknowledgement log or current health state. Source defaults, authored example settings and successful manual commands **are not measured effective state**. The eventual meaning of configuration, status, action and lifecycle must be justified separately rather than inferred from available fields or familiar UI tabs.

These are **analyst comparative findings based on cited source evidence**, not new adopted DIM entities, a mandatory five-piece structure, interface rules, formal conformance tests, or evidence of full cross-vendor compatibility.

## 14. Step 10 — Three competing ways to bound the whole sensing System (NOT a chassis decision)

**Status: Step 10 COMPLETE, 1 November 2026. Phase C exploratory comparison only; Step 11 NEXT.** This step derives **three deliberately competing boundary hypotheses** from the **four source-native cases** in [§§9–12](#9-step-05--dragino-s31-lb-source-native-reference-system) and the [neutral Step 09 comparison (§13)](#13-step-09--four-source-native-arrangements-compared-without-choosing-a-chassis). They are **analyst-generated, testable alternatives**, not definitions found verbatim in any standard, approved DIM classes, a final “chassis,” an application Profile, or three modules that must all appear in the eventual architecture.

**The question:** When a Profile-aware client displays “this System,” *what makes that referent one coherent whole*? Is it the **physical unit**, the **set of sensing/processing/reporting roles producing a result**, or the **installation that persists as equipment comes and goes**? Different original sources emphasize different answers. The eventual DIM may require a different formulation, multiple distinguishable perspectives, or a fourth alternative introduced by the donors. **Do not privilege a familiar V1 name, the prior F-01–F-05 candidate meanings, or a CSAPI resource type as the answer.**

### 14.1 Three distinct provisional boundaries

| Alternative boundary — **working label only** | What would identify one “whole” | Relationships it **must be able to express** | What is initially *outside* that boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Physical equipment unit or assembled product** | An identified hardware unit/assembly (or, without a real unit, a manufacturer-defined product arrangement): enclosure, node, logger, or instrument as the chosen physical referent. | Individual unit vs model; **physical constituent** vs separately connected equipment; interfaces; sensing element vs output property/channel; support electronics; hardware revision; condition/configuration **for the correct particular unit**; external hosts and installations as **associated**, not automatically contained. | Separate logger, gateway, station, archive or network service **unless the physical boundary is explicitly a manufactured assembly containing it**. A connection alone does not make a component internal. |
| **B — An observing and reporting arrangement** | The bounded arrangement that performs a stated sensing/reporting **function or result-producing responsibility**; may use multiple individually identifiable instruments, processors and transports. | Which actor actually senses, which acquires/processes, which retains/publishes; physical/logical/communication associations; supported method vs executed activity; input-to-output transformations; freshness, quality and times; support configuration and controls; **multiple functions on shared equipment**. | Other unrelated capabilities, independent equipment lifecycles and infrastructure **not needed** for the chosen reporting responsibility. The boundary depends on which function/report is chosen and cannot be inferred from a value or DataStream alone. |
| **C — A continuing monitoring installation or station** | A site-, installation- or operator-defined monitoring arrangement that persists through some equipment changes and whose continuity is established by a station/installation identifier and dated history. | Persistent station identity; role assignments to distinct serial-numbered hardware **over intervals**; installation/host relationships; environmental subject/site; logger/transmitter/reporting; replacements, maintenance and calibration history; provenance of historical station reports. | Uninstalled product types, unsited portable devices and equipment operating outside a named continuing installation **unless separately related**. A product's ability to be installed does **not** create a documented station. |

**These are competing answers to the scope question, not automatically mutually exclusive real-world objects.** A physical instrument can serve in an installation and participate in an observing arrangement. But **choosing one as the primary organizing referent** changes what a common client identifies first, what remains contextual, and which data become misleading if silently attached to that referent. The alternatives are therefore genuine *modeling choices to investigate*, not three synonyms for “System.” Their use here is **CANDIDATE_MAPPING/analyst hypothesis**, not SOURCE_FACT or normative OGC language.

A candidate must fail or remain **NOT TESTED** where it requires missing evidence, rather than being declared correct because we filled the gaps with plausible details.

### 14.3 What would disprove or substantially weaken each alternative?

- **A (physical-unit primary):** If a client using only that root cannot faithfully describe a documented **separate logger + sensing assembly** without falsely making one a constituent of the other; or cannot distinguish **Bennett's continuing station** from changing probe identity, it fails the desired **whole-System representation** under that scope. It can still be a valid **equipment subview**; the failure would be treating that view as the **exclusive organizing subject**.
- **B (observing/reporting-function primary):** If the same device's **power, configuration, command/response, communications, maintenance and physical individuality** disappear whenever a client focuses on one output, or if the model invents a service/process link not established by source data, it fails **whole-System coverage/source fidelity**. If a single sensing function is not well defined in a manufacturer reference, do **not** create a fielded process instance from a product spec.
- **C (persistent-station primary):** If the client has to invent a **deployment or site identity** before it can use a perfectly documented uninstalled S31-LB, AirGradient or ClimaVue product/reference arrangement, it fails the **product/design-level use cases**. If the station root silently makes all attached physical units interchangeable or converts operator use-end dates into exact deployment endpoints, it fails **Bennett's actual historical evidence**.

**Possible but not chosen:** the standards may support **separately represented equipment, functions and installation associations** with one *declared subject of interest* per client request rather than one universal all-purpose root. This is another **hypothesis to examine**, **not** permission to declare the problem solved by “use all three,” draft three mandatory classes, or assume every source naturally supplies all three perspectives. The exact boundary, identity, cardinality, composition/association semantics and availability of evidence remain unresolved.

### 15.1 In ordinary language — the original donors contribute different things

**AAS first asks what asset is being digitally described.** The **Asset Administration Shell** has a unique identity as a description/representation; the asset it represents has its own kind and asset identifiers. AAS makes a **type asset** different from an **instance asset**, and allows a *role* asset kind; **Submodels** describe asset aspects, while **Entity** and **RelationshipElement** can represent constituent items and explicit relations. This is potentially useful for equipment identity and representation, **but the AAS Shell itself is not the physical probe, logger or station**. It is also not a universally accepted temporal Deployment model. [Original AAS Shell and AssetInformation metamodel](https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/aas-specifications/IDTA-01001/v3.2/spec-metamodel/core.html); [types and instances](https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/aas-specifications/IDTA-01001/v3.2/annex/general.html); [Entity and RelationshipElement](https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/aas-specifications/IDTA-01001/v3.2/spec-metamodel/submodel-elements.html).

**OPC UA Devices starts with a Device and its information/topology representation.** The standard defines a Device in real-world terms, explicitly allows a Device to be a topology of other Devices or parts, and gives **ComponentType** a broader range than **DeviceType**, including **software components**. It distinguishes the vendor's **Model** nameplate information from **DeviceType**, which is an OPC UA ObjectType defining the **representation's structure**. **ConnectsTo** documents a communication/topological relation, **not universal mechanical containment**. Separate **offline and online Objects** may expose configuration versus real device parameters; **offline configuration is not evidence that a field unit is currently in that state**. [Original Devices §§3.1.5, 4.1, 4.5.2, 4.6–4.7, 5.5, 6.1–6.3, 9.4](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-10000-100/).

**PA-DIM is not just another name for Devices.** **PADIMType** is a **subtype of Devices ComponentType**, **not** a subtype of Devices **DeviceType**. Its source [§7.2, Table 109](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-30081/7.2) makes specific inherited nameplate fields mandatory within its PA-DIM model, applies device-health and administration/signal interfaces, and defines optional **SignalSet**, **SubDevices**, and **DeviceConditionSet** structures. **Signals, process variables, subdevices and condition variables serve different native purposes**. PA-DIM also links types/properties to **IEC 61987 Common Data Dictionary** entries through **IRDI identifiers**; those provide semantic vocabulary, not proof any selected equipment complies with PA-DIM. [Original PA-DIM §§6.1, 6.3, 7.1.2, 7.2, 7.22, 7.24–7.25](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-30081/6.1).

## 16. Step 12 — ISA 6.0 and The Open Group SOSA: physical, functional, visible and managed relationships

**Status: Step 12 COMPLETE, 1 November 2026. Step 13 NEXT. Gate C remains OPEN.** We reread **four preserved primary PDFs** at their exact original passages: Army **Integrated Sensor Architecture (ISA) Software Design Document, 6.0 revision 5 (31 October 2023)**; **ISA Data Model Specification, Release 6.0 revision 12 (31 October 2023)**; **The Open Group Sensor Open Systems Architecture (SOSA) Reference Architecture Edition 1.1, C239 (June 2023, September erratum)**; and the **separate Edition 1.0 SOSA Domain-Specific Data Model (April 2022)**. Original source evidence is distinguished from the project’s proposed applicability. This is **not** the separate W3C/OGC Sensor, Observation, Sample, and Actuator / Semantic Sensor Network (SOSA/SSN) lineage, which belongs in Step 14.

**Why the donor exercise matters:** One source chiefly describes **who can be discovered, what it exposes, and which actions can reach it through software**; the other explicitly describes a **bounded Sensor** with physical, functional, product/implementation, hosting and management distinctions. A common client that treats every advertised network participant as one identified physical sensor—or every function and manager as one internal hardware part—would misstate the source. **Neither source thereby settles the DIM chassis** or proves the three manufacturer reference cases implement the standards. The earlier [ISA/SOSA challenge research](../Donor_Challenge/04_Other_Structural_Challenges.md) is preserved for historical comparison, but the findings below were **checked afresh from the original documents**, without the old obligation to defend F-01 through F-05.

### 16.1 What ISA actually describes

The **ISA Software Design Document §3 (printed pp. 6–7; PDF pp. 14–15)** explicitly describes ISA as a **software-only interoperability solution** that relies on the host platform’s networks, computing and authoritative time source. In **§4.2.1 (printed p. 9; PDF p. 17)**, an **ISA Component** implements the ISA interface and participates in ISA services; an **ISA Controller** is a component providing core services to connected participants. A joined network participant is **not by definition a physical sensing chip, a piece of equipment, or a separately serialized unit**.

The decisive passage is **§4.2.2 (printed pp. 10–12; PDF pp. 18–20), including Figure 2**, visually rechecked in the original PDF. An **ISA Composition** reaches the wider network through a **Composition Interface Component (CIC)**. Composition members may use ISA or their native interfaces. A **visible member** is announced to the network with its own capabilities/status; an **opaque member** is not announced, and its exposed capabilities appear as the CIC’s. A visible non-ISA member may be a **virtual ISA Component**, with an ISA identifier assigned in relation to its CIC. Of the visible members, **transparent** means externally taskable and **translucent** means visible but not externally taskable. The CIC can itself execute, broker, or relay a requested task. **Visibility, taskability, reporting source, interface identity and actual physical equipment are different source-level questions**. They are not synonyms for contained-versus-external, installed-versus-uninstalled, or healthy-versus-faulted.

**ISA's Component Capability Description (CCD)** is explicitly described in **§5.1.1 (printed pp. 15–16; PDF pp. 23–24)**. A component **declares Properties** about information it can report regarding itself, **Observables** describing observation information it can publish, and **Commands** it can be asked to execute. It also reports capability **ready** and where applicable **reporting** states; actual **Status** messages carry property values, while Requests and Responses concern commands. These are not all one “sensor characteristic,” actual observation, executed command or current equipment condition.

The original **ISA Data Model Specification, release 6.0 revision 12**, lists **Properties / Observables / Commands** by service and **Capability Bins (§2, printed pp. 3–8; PDF pp. 18–23)**. Examples include entity detection, meteorology, electrical and general-purpose capabilities. Its **Document Notes (PDF p. 2)** independently distinguish extension markings **Custom**, **Nominated** and **Accepted**; **Accepted** means suggested for use and expected to transition later, **not already standardized in that release**. Even a primary PDF therefore cannot be mined as if every listed extension had uniform normative standing.

### 16.2 What The Open Group SOSA actually describes

In **Edition 1.1 §4.1.1.1–.4 (C239, printed pp. 21–22; PDF pp. 47–48)**, the primary **SOSA Sensor** is made of **one or more Sensor Hardware Elements**, which may be physically grouped or distributed **on or within its host platform**. A **Host Platform** is an associated **vehicle/structure/pod** which may supply power, cooling, navigation, network and tasking. The platform may also contain **processing outside the Sensor boundary**. The document restricts its Sensor types to **Communications, Electro-Optical/Infrared, Electronic Warfare, Radar and Signals Intelligence**, individually or combined. Thus **SOSA's whole Sensor is compelling source-native scope evidence**, but not a general definition that automatically covers our environmental monitors, or a claim that every mount/platform is a part of the sensor.

The **Edition 1.1 §4.2, Table 4.2-1 (printed pp. 22–24; PDF pp. 48–50)** and **§5.1 (printed pp. 29–30; PDF pp. 55–56)** separate **logical SOSA Modules**, which encapsulate functions, behavior and interactions, from **SOSA Infrastructure**, which hosts execution and interconnects. One SOSA Sensor may implement only the relevant subset of Modules; it need not produce one physical board or system resource per Module. The **System Manager** handles Sensor hardware/software discovery, configuration, control, health and security; the **Task Manager** coordinates **mission** tasking, priorities and outputs. These source-defined management and mission roles are distinguishable from one another and from the physical device itself.

**Edition 1.1 §6 and §6.1 (printed pp. 38–39; PDF pp. 64–65)** distinguishes **managed** from **unmanaged** components and permits **indirect management**, where a second component implements a management interface for the first. It also explicitly separates **in-band management** over the normal message interconnect from **out-of-band hardware management** that can remain available when the normal network is unavailable. **Who exposes management status need not be the physical component whose status is being managed**, nor does an available System Manager imply that a specific bit of health data was obtained.

The **Edition 1.0 Domain-Specific Data Model (DSDM)** provides additional **edition-specific** relationship definitions, **not automatically Edition 1.1 cardinalities**:

- **§1.1.3.15, printed p. 13 (PDF p. 13):** **Component_** is a physical **or software** unit with functional boundaries, purpose and interfaces. **§§1.1.3.68–.69, pp. 36–37:** **Module_** and **ModuleImplementation** distinguish defined functional units from executable implementation association.
- **§1.1.3.82–.83, printed p. 42:** **Product** is a manufacturer’s hardware or software product; **ProductInstance** associates a manufactured Product with a **functional Component_**. A manufactured product, functional Component and executable ModuleImplementation are **not the same source type**.
- **§1.1.3.54, printed pp. 30–31:** **HostPlatform** associates a Sensor with a Platform; **§1.1.3.92, printed p. 46:** **SensorComponent** expresses **component is part of Sensor**. These are **two different relationship kinds**, with original DSDM cardinalities attached to **that original edition only**.
- **§§1.1.3.89–.91, printed pp. 44–46:** the **Sensor** carries separate **healthStatus, operationalState and configurationState**, while **SensorBuiltInTest** and **SensorBITResult** separately concern a test’s association and its **executed, dated result**. Health status, configuration, fault, mission task and executed built-in-test result are not one scalar meaning.

### 16.4 How this donor evidence challenges the OPEN boundaries and the four cases

**A — primary physical equipment unit/assembly.** SOSA's **multi-element Sensor** is a serious **native alternative** to an always-single-enclosure definition. Its distinction between a Sensor and Host Platform prevents automatically treating the mounted host as a manufactured sensor part. Yet SOSA's specific five-modality scope and technical conformance architecture mean it **cannot simply become the V2 universal chassis**. ISA adds a different challenge: a network-visible Component **does not have to correspond one-to-one to physical equipment**. Choosing equipment as the primary referent would still need independent discovery/management representation, physical type-versus-instance evidence, and externally attached components.

**B — observing/reporting arrangement.** ISA's CIC can **broker or relay** capabilities and present a member opaquely or virtually; its CCD can declare observables and commands distinct from actual reports. SOSA separates **functional Module**, realization by Component, **System Manager versus Task Manager**, and Sensor data path. These strengthen the need to distinguish **who observed, who exposed the capability, who processed/published the result, and who accepted a command**. However, neither inspected source establishes a general **station-led versus probe-led OGC DataStream.system** rule, exact physical-observer provenance for all managed outputs, or runtime conformance of our Glaux case. The boundary hypothesis cannot be accepted by borrowing these interface names.

**C — continuing installation/station.** SOSA Host Platform is **associated but distinct** from a SOSA Sensor; ISA's platform hosts physical infrastructure for interface participants. Neither is by itself equivalent to a **persistent environmental monitoring station with dated instrument role replacements** like Bennett's Point. The DSDM ProductInstance and SensorComponent relationships do not supply an automatic **effective-from/effective-to** equipment history, and an opaque/virtual ISA identity is not a durable unit serial. Station continuity, physical replacement/role intervals and actual history **remain independent issues for later evidence/donor review**.

**Additional open dimensions not reducible to A/B/C:** source-native **external exposure versus physical existence** (ISA); **functional module versus physical/software implementation versus manufactured Product** (SOSA DSDM); **hosting versus constituent membership** (SOSA); **system housekeeping versus mission/task behavior**, including **direct/indirect management** (SOSA); and the provenance of **the reporter/relay versus actual equipment subject** (ISA and SOSA). These are **meaning questions, not five new classes or an automatically winning hybrid**. Their effect on final architecture must await the rest of Phase C.

### 17.1 What we learned in ordinary language

**IEEE 1451 helps us understand a sensing channel and its technical self-description, not automatically an entire installed System.** Its currently published architecture separates the **Application (APP)** as network client, **Network Capable Application Processor (NCAP)** as network server, and **Transducer Interface Module (TIM)** on the transducer side. The same published scope distinguishes network services, transducer services, **Transducer Electronic Data Sheets (TEDS)**, and reading or controlling actual transducer data. This architecture has genuine relevance to sensing devices and their communication, but **an APP, NCAP, TIM, transducer channel, physical sensing element and observed property are not six interchangeable physical equipment identities**. [IEEE Standards Association, IEEE 1451.0-2024, published 26 June 2024, public scope](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1451.0/11001/).

**The public technical depth is substantial.** The [IEEE 1451 Playground Transducer Channel TEDS page](https://iml.ubi.pt/playground/docs/ieee1451/teds/transducer-channel-teds/), which explicitly cites the 2024 standard's **§6.5 and Table 19**, enumerates channel type, calibration key, physical units, design measurement limits, worst-case uncertainty, self-test, sample representation, sampling periods, delays, timing source, timing error, modes and buffering. Its [Meta TEDS page](https://iml.ubi.pt/playground/docs/ieee1451/teds/meta-teds/) cites **§6.4 and Tables 13, 49–50** and documents a module-level UUID, maximum channel count, control/vector grouping and channel proxies. [NIST's 1 December 2024 news report](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/12/nist-and-university-researchers-present-online-tool-support-ieee-standards) explicitly identifies the Playground as an IEEE 1451.0-2024 teaching/validation tool; its [2025 IEEE publication record](https://www.nist.gov/publications/ieee-1451-playground-web-based-tool-standard-education) independently documents its development and testing. **These original current-revision-linked sources are legitimate detailed technical donors.** They are **not** a complete independent reading of the licensed 2024 normative SHALLs or a fielded TIM/TEDS test.

**SensorML 3.0 contributes a broader process/system-description mechanism.** Its core model treats hardware detectors, physical sensor systems, and computational transformations as **processes with identified inputs, outputs, parameters and methodology**. It distinguishes **SimpleProcess**, **AggregateProcess**, **PhysicalComponent**, and **PhysicalSystem**, supplies **typeOf** inheritance to a more general process description, supports configuration and modes, uses **SWE Common** data structures, and has separate **history, validTime and Deployment** mechanisms. This makes it possible to ask where technical channel descriptions might fit while preserving a multi-equipment system or measurement-processing chain. **It does not itself declare an IEEE TEDS field to be a SensorML item, nor resolve the physical observer/CSAPI DataStream producer or generate actual Observations.** [OGC 23-000, version 3.0, approved 2 June 2025 and published 16 July 2025](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-000/23-000.html).

### 17.3 IEEE 1451 — module, addressed channel, technical metadata and actual readings

**Source-native architecture:** The IEEE publisher's scope assigns **network-facing APP/NCAP interactions** and **NCAP-to-TIM transducer services** different responsibilities. Reading a TEDS is different from reading actual sampled data; setup/control services are different from successfully performing a requested action. A TIM can have **multiple addressed channels** within the source description, and the Playground explicitly describes shared Meta TEDS information and channel-specific TEDS. **This does not prove a TIM is one chip, every physical sensing element is one channel, a channel is an independently managed unit, or a channel is one CSAPI DataStream.**

### 17.4 SensorML 3.0 — a broad process model, not a renamed IEEE hierarchy

**SensorML's normative conceptual unit is a Process.** Under **§§7.1–7.2, Requirements 2–5**, its process descriptions concern **inputs, outputs, parameters, methodology, unique identification and discovery metadata**. A physical detector can take an environmental **ObservableProperty** as input and yield a SWE Common **Quantity**, while a mathematical correction can be a non-physical process. That allows descriptions of an actual sensing path without requiring one output per physical child. **SensorML itself is a process-description standard; actual observations and data streams remain distinct information artifacts**. [Original SensorML §§7.1–7.2](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-000/23-000.html#_process_definitions); [§8.2.9.1 and Requirement 11](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-000/23-000.html#_inputs_outputs_and_parameters).

### 17.6 What is a real alignment, what is merely analogous, and what stays open?

**Supported directly as concepts in the separately cited originals:**

1. IEEE 1451 has **network-side and transducer-side service roles** plus **TEDS**; Playground current-linked sources have **Meta** (module-level) and **TransducerChannel** metadata with granular technical attributes.
2. SensorML has **identified process descriptions, type/instance reference, physical/logical process distinctions, inputs/outputs/parameters, process methods, configuration, validity/history and deployment**.
3. Both bodies supply useful ways to think about the **described subject and data semantics**, but **neither original source checked here declares an exact IEEE Channel → SensorML Component/Output/Capability/Procedure/CSAPI DataStream mapping**. The public IEEE source does not require that our model be reorganized around NCAP/TIM; SensorML source semantics are broader and need actual property definitions.

**The new problem these sources make visible:** **how to preserve a small, technically rich, channel-level description and its owning physical/contextual scope inside a genuinely whole-System model**. The sensor itself, its measured property, addressable channel, self-description, sampled signal, process, and API resource have **different potential identities**. A common client should not force one of them to stand for all the others. This is an empirically grounded challenge, **not an eighth universal DIM class or a completed solution**.

### 18.1 What the four donors contribute in ordinary language

**SAPIENT v2 describes message/network participation and operational actions.** A Node registers capabilities, supported modes, task definitions, status reporting and equipment information; later StatusReport, Task, TaskAck, DetectionReport and Alert messages describe different kinds of activity. The message-sender UUID **is not automatically a physical serial number or the observer**, especially when a Node can be fusion/software/platform equipment. A supported Task is distinct from a requested, accepted, completed or failed Task. This is a bounded **interface control specification**, not a universal physical-system ontology. [BSI Flex 335 v2.0](https://www.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/en/insights-and-media/insights/brochures/bsi-flex-335.pdf), §§3.1, 4.1–4.11 and 6.2–6.6.

**OMS 3.0 describes an Observation as an actual performed act.** An identifiable **Observer** performs an Observation using a reusable **ObservingProcedure** to determine the **ObservableProperty** of a **featureOfInterest**, returning a **result**. A **Host** can group observers (including at a monitoring facility), and **Deployment** assigns Observer to Host. OMS says **phenomenonTime** is when the result applies, including the interval for an average; **resultTime** is when the result became available. These are not a logger, probe, timestamp, procedure or observed environmental subject all bearing the same identity. [OGC 20-082r4 / OMS 3.0](https://docs.ogc.org/as/20-082r4/20-082r4.html), original §§8.2.2–8.2.11 and 8.4–8.8.

**W3C/OGC SOSA-SSN gives formal relationships for systems, sensors, observations and deployment.** The **19 October 2017 Recommendation** has SOSA/SSN classes and relations including Sensor, Observation, Procedure, System, Platform, Deployment, madeBySensor, usedProcedure, implements, hasSubSystem and isHostedBy. It explicitly permits sensor-like software and human observers in its scope. The newer **“2023 Edition” has a 26 October 2026 W3C Working Draft**, with proposed Asset/System/Deployment organization, a closer OMS RDF extension and revised term/namespace arrangements. **That draft is not a new Recommendation or a revision already binding CSAPI Part 1/Part 2.** [2017 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/2017/REC-vocab-ssn-20171019/), [W3C edition/status history](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-ssn/all/), [dated 2026 draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-vocab-ssn-2023-20261026/).

**WoT separates a Thing from its description and a potentially different virtual/proxy identity.** **Thing Description 1.1** describes reusable **Property, Action and Event interaction affordances**, data schemas, HTTP/other protocol Forms, security metadata and links; **Thing Model** describes a template that may lack any fielded endpoint or instance serial. **WoT Architecture 1.1** describes proxy **Intermediaries** that may expose another Thing under a different digital ID, and **Digital Twins** that may cache values, queue commands or aggregate several Things. **A client receiving information does not thereby know which physical sensor produced it or whether a reported value is fresh.** [Thing Description 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/2023/REC-wot-thing-description11-20231205/), §§1, 5–6 and 9; [WoT Architecture 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/2023/REC-wot-architecture11-20231205/), §§5.7 and 9.2.2.

**Keep acronyms separate:** W3C/OGC **Sensor, Observation, Sample, and Actuator (SOSA)** and **Semantic Sensor Network (SSN)** are a **different lineage** from **The Open Group Sensor Open Systems Architecture (SOSA)** reviewed in [Step 12](#16-step-12--isa-60-and-the-open-group-sosa-physical-functional-visible-and-managed-relationships). No shared acronym establishes a formal equivalence or adoption.

### 18.5 Consequences for the three OPEN Step 10 boundaries

**A — physical equipment primary.** OMS directly distinguishes the **individual Observer** from reusable methods; SSN models Systems/Sensors, but permits non-hardware observers, while SAPIENT Node may be software/fusion. WoT proxies can use new IDs. A purely physical-root model risks losing **who actually performed or reported an event**. Yet none of these authors requires a distinct physical System for each observation or network message. **A stays OPEN.**

**B — observing/reporting arrangement primary.** OMS gives the strongest **Observer + ObservingProcedure + Observation + feature/property/result/time** semantics, and SSN gives related ontology links. SAPIENT distinguishes **capability versus selected state versus Task/TaskAck**, while WoT supplies Property/Action/Event affordances and intermediaries. This improves an arrangement-oriented model but also creates **multiple noninterchangeable participants**: physical sensor, logger, reporting Node, proxy/digital twin, publisher and actual observer. No reviewed source settles **CSAPI DataStream.system ownership**. **B stays OPEN.**

**C — continuing installation/station primary.** OMS's **Host** can describe an actual environmental monitoring facility and its **Deployment** can express Observer-to-Host association. The SSN 2017 ontology includes hosting/deployment; the 2026 draft proposes an Asset-centered improvement. This is useful for Bennett's station continuity, but **cannot be imposed on uninstalled reference products**. A Host can be an organizational survey, not just a physical enclosure. **C stays OPEN.**

**Additional donor-led possibility, not a chosen fourth chassis:** one could keep distinct **equipment, observing activity, host/installation and reporting/mediation referents**, with relationships and source authority explicitly qualified, rather than picking a unique universal object that all fields attach to. Such a flexible model could also become vague and untestable. **Step 15 must evaluate this possibility against specific source evidence and acceptance tests before any choice.**

### 19.1 Plain-language decision: what is the whole we are describing?

**No single manufactured enclosure, observation stream/function, or permanent monitoring station is the universal whole.** Dragino describes a radio node plus an external probe; AirGradient describes a multi-device assembled monitor; Campbell describes a separate logger and weather instrument working together; Bennett documents an actual persistent station with replaceable probes. None can be forced into one of those three exclusive roots without fabricating a unit, suppressing a real relationship, or confusing a reporter with its physical source.

**Working subject:** a **source-grounded, bounded, equipment-realized sensing System/arrangement**, whose defining purpose includes sensing/observing and whose relevant equipment, observing/processing, communications, power, configuration, management, reporting and life-cycle relations can be described **only as the source evidence warrants**. The source must establish **which subject is in focus** and its standing: **reference product/arrangement**, **actual identified individual equipment**, or **actual identified station/installation**. **A reference arrangement is not an authenticated fielded System instance**. This is a provisional description of our focus, **not a minted domain name or adopted superclass**; retain the original provisional “system and sensor and equipment” description until justified.

**Selected working approach D — a declared focal subject with separately identifiable, typed, evidence-qualified relationships.** A description focuses on one source-declared whole or reference arrangement and preserves other physical units, observing roles, hosts, processors, managers, publishers/proxies and source records **as distinct, only when supported**. It does **not** require every focus to instantiate all of those views; it does **not** define a generic graph of everything; and it **does not** assert a physical system from an API resource ID. This is the **preferred structural method for drafting**, subject to falsification in Steps 16–24, rather than choosing an irrevocable single DIM root.

### 19.5 Bounded inclusion, exclusion and falsification rules for the draft

The following constrain **future research interpretation**, not final DIM class definitions or mandatory Profile attributes.

1. **State the focus and evidence standing.** Distinguish product or reference arrangement from a real individually identified physical unit and a source-identified station. **No real System instance is inferred from a manufacturer PDF, mock serial, publisher code example or API placeholder.**
2. **State inclusion by original evidence and relevance.** Include source-described sensing hardware, processor/logger, functional/communication/power equipment, software/firmware, control, quality/condition, maintenance and provenance as **linked roles and descriptions when relevant to this bounded sensing focus**. Do not require any such facet merely because a standards catalog or UI tab lists it.
3. **Do not collapse relations.** Physical constituent, separately connected, hosted/deployed, implements/performs, observes, computes/averages, published/reported/proxied by, managed by and served in a role during an interval are **different questions**. No mandatory DIM edge names or cardinalities are adopted here.
4. **Distinguish identity domains.** Manufacturer model, actual physical serial, platform/station ID, SAPIENT node UUID, IEEE transducer/channel ID, SDI-12 address, CSAPI resource ID, Thing/description/proxy identifier and information-record ID are **not self-evidently the same individual**. The provider/source of a value is not necessarily the observer.
5. **Separate capability, actual configuration, activity and report.** A specification declares what can happen. An installed setting/condition requires operating evidence. A sent Task, accepted Task, completed outcome and sensor Observation are different source propositions, as are environmental record QA and hardware health.
6. **Treat missing history and time honestly.** Bennett's two selected averages are bounded historical evidence, and their nominal 15-minute intervals/probe attributions are project derived. Source data do **not** identify exact probe replacement minute, independently measured probe coordinates, true resultTime or complete running system configuration.
7. **Keep model, Profile and client work separate.** The DIM describes meanings and relations without choosing a JSON encoding, CSAPI System vs DataStream owner, SensorML class for every constituent, W3C draft namespace or Glaux UI layout. Later Profile and implementation work must demonstrate actual meaning preservation. **A successful one-case backend readback does not prove cross-vendor interoperability.**

**First-draft primary-subject exclusions:** generic enterprise IT, whole industrial plants unrelated to sensing, a complete mission/asset-management enterprise, actuator-only systems, entirely virtual Things, standalone software or human survey campaigns, and all possible security/maintenance infrastructures **are not automatically equal primary focal Systems in this scoped draft**. Their **real relationships to sensing equipment** still deserve representation where the source supports them. These exclusions are bounded **release-scope choices**, not claims those domains are unimportant or impossible or that OMS/SSN human/software observers are invalid.

**Overturn or restrict D if:** (a) a fielded source demands another referent and D cannot preserve it without fabricated hardware; (b) a simpler donor-founded structure accurately fits all four cases with fewer unsupported assumptions; (c) the integrated model creates circular System identities or converts a documented reference pairing into a fielded station; (d) the semantics are so generic that independent producer/observer/host/equipment interpretation is impossible; or (e) a source→DIM→Profile→client test demonstrably loses critical original configuration, observation provenance/time or hardware-state meaning. **Failure is grounds to revise D, not to relabel a source to fit it.**

**Naming guard:** retain provisional **system and sensor and equipment** language. This working frame is **not** a renamed “managed physical sensing system,” the incumbent five F-01–F-05 slots, a power-first taxonomy, a copy of SOSA's defense Sensor class, or a fixed UI category list.

### 20.1 In ordinary language — why this is a real modeling distinction

**A real piece of equipment is not its catalogue name, and neither is its digital record.** Bennett's Point offers a clear example: **EE181** names a product kind, two different **serial numbers** identify two physical probes, **acebpmet** identifies the *continuing monitoring station*, and the source NOAA **ATemp** rows identify particular historical station-temperature reports but **do not name the probes**. Our probe attribution uses separate dated equipment-use records and is therefore **DERIVED**, not an original source field. A digital API can show a representation of each subject, but its local resource identifier is **not automatically the corresponding physical serial or station code**. [Bennett original case and qualifying sources (§12)](#12-step-08--bennetts-point-actual-station-time-scoped-probes-and-historical-temperature-reports); [pinned selected source record](../Candidate_Specification/examples/bennetts-point-ee181.json).

**Dragino demonstrates the negative case:** we know a manufacturer-specified **S31-LB node and external SHT31 probe** and a decoder that emits the **Node_type** label, but we have **no authenticated real S31-LB physical unit or EUI**. Neither a product kind nor a decoder field establishes an actual fielded individual. **The same is true of AirGradient's published placeholder serial/0.0 coordinates and Campbell's example loggerID or ClimaVue SN-yyyyyyyy**. Thus a useful DIM must describe reusable designs and connected reference arrangements **without manufacturing station/unit objects to fill a UI**. [Dragino source sheet](../Controlled_Corpus/System_Data_Sheets/Dragino_S31_LB.md); [AirGradient source sheet](../Controlled_Corpus/System_Data_Sheets/AirGradient_Open_Air_Max.md); [Campbell source sheet](../Controlled_Corpus/System_Data_Sheets/Campbell_CR1000X_ClimaVue50.md).

## 21. Step 17 — Physical constituents, connected equipment, functions and hosting

**Status: Step 17 COMPLETE, 2 November 2026. Gate D OPEN; Step 18 NEXT.** The [actual candidate DIM — Step 17 physical/functional relationship section](../Candidate_Specification/DIM.md#step-17--physical-constituent-external-connection-functional-realization-and-hosting) is the **single model deliverable**. This workbook entry documents the positive and negative evidence checks. We used source-native equipment arrangements from [§§9–12](#9-step-05--dragino-s31-lb-source-native-reference-system), plus the independently reviewed **OPC UA Devices, AAS, SensorML, The Open Group SOSA, ISA and OMS** donor distinctions in §§15–18. These materials are not combined into a new universal mandatory relation ontology.

### 21.1 The physical distinction in plain language

**A component is not defined by how many data values it reports. A connection is not the same as physical containment. A functional role is not necessarily one physical item. A station is not merely a permanently assembled product.**

The original examples are deliberately different: **Dragino** sells a radio/node product with an **external three-metre SHT31 probe**; **AirGradient** describes a manufactured enclosure and main circuit board with **two directly attached PMS5003 sensors and several plug-in sensing boards**; **Campbell** documents **two separate products** joined over **SDI-12**; and **Bennett's Point** records a continuing *operational station* with **two different serialized EE181 probes used during different periods**. If we give all those connections one unqualified “has part” arrow, we erase source meaning or claim a fielded installation that was never observed.

The physical and functional distinctions may **coexist** rather than compete. AirGradient's **SHT4x module** is a documented physical product constituent, communicates through I²C, and serves a measurement role; those are not the same proposition. Dragino's probe can be **supplied as part of the reference product** and **external to the node's housing**. Campbell's data logger can **acquire and process the weather sensor's outputs** without the ClimaVue being manufactured inside the logger. Such statements require the original source and its design-versus-individual standing.

### 21.3 Source-donor checks rather than a copied standard taxonomy

The [original OPC UA Devices v1.05.0 §5.5](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/specs/OPC-10000-100/5.5) explicitly defines **ConnectsTo** as a **symmetric NonHierarchical network-topology relation**; this is independent of the Device Model's physical/software component structure and **Device Integration Host**. [AAS Part 1 v3.2](https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/aas-specifications/IDTA-01001/v3.2/spec-metamodel/submodel-elements.html) distinguishes an Entity in a composite asset representation from its management granularity, not from an externally authenticated physical serial. [SensorML 3.0 §§7.1–7.2, 8.4–8.6](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-000/23-000.html) can represent **physical and computational processes and their connections**, but that process graph cannot be assumed to be the same as the equipment's physical assembly or ownership tree.

The **Open Group SOSA** Sensor–Host Platform and functional Module–implementation relationships, **ISA** visible/opaque/virtual interface-member relationships, and **OMS** Observer–Host–Deployment concepts supply further **different meanings of hosting, participation and management**. See source-specific qualified research [§16](#16-step-12--isa-60-and-the-open-group-sosa-physical-functional-visible-and-managed-relationships) and [§18](#18-step-14--sapient-v2-oms-30-w3cogc-sosa-ssn-and-web-of-things-observation-action-time-and-representation). They **do not jointly publish a single mandatory DIM “part-of” rule**. A source declaring visibility, status or hosting cannot be promoted to physical membership by analogy.

### 21.4 Negative checks and the Step 17 decision boundary

- **Campbell:** reject “ClimaVue is an intrinsic logger component” and “the 2019 example program and 2026 MQTT logger status establish a joined deployed station”; preserve **two physical product roles connected at design level**.
