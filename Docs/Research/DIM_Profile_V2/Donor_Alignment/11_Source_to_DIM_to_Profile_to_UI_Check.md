## 1. Answer to the original question

**The five-meaning candidate foundation helps prevent several demonstrated source-to-client misunderstandings, but it does not yet provide the complete semantic coverage, versioned Profile binding, producer transformation, or client interpretation needed to prove the original shared System Details objective.** The prototype is a useful first **illustration** that one display frame can organize selected TRSS, OmniSense and Smart Citizen descriptions into more meaningful groupings. Its meaningful positive evidence is **source-qualified separation**: installed versus supported selections, estimate versus reported voltage, product design versus actual individual, physical chip versus measured channel, reported labels versus authenticated part identities, and digital database association versus hardware.

**The stronger interoperability claim remains UNTESTED.** The Smart Citizen input is 20 intentionally authored **product-design facts**, not a retrieved physical System. TRSS and the two OmniSense inputs are **synthetic SensorML-shaped transcriptions of historical examples**, not native manufacturer payloads. The candidate term vocabulary is an implementation-specific presentation experiment, **not** an adopted DIM/CSAPI Profile. There are no profile-constrained real CSAPI responses, authenticated individual devices, actual observations or browser-verified source-to-UI fidelity tests for these cases. Earlier research expressly warned that a generic CSAPI/SensorML **representation** can retain source semantics while a generic **renderer** fails to interpret them; this study does not establish that CSAPI deleted any vendor information ([original baseline](../DIM_Profile_V2_Problem_and_Outcome_Baseline_v0.1_28OCT2026.md); [profile test design](https://github.com/OS4CSAPI/glaux-client/blob/work/details-view-20261023/Docs/Design/RESOURCE_DETAILS_PROFILE_TEST.md)).

An additional **code-level finding** matters for future Profile design: the deployed prototype uses the **last segment of a source definition URI** as its term key and then finds a hand-authored display rule. It does not validate the **full term URI, namespace/version, group definition, intended carrier/type or code space** in this particular four-case renderer. Thus a matching suffix from an *unrecognized* source namespace could be misinterpreted. This is a **static code-path risk identified in this review, not an executed hostile-payload test** ([deployed source rulebook](https://github.com/OS4CSAPI/glaux-client/blob/61bff8ecc4aa40dec39975878e098826d50580a4/src/profile/resource-details-proof.ts#L162-L215)).

The code-level gaps are **candidate integration and interpreter requirements research**, not defects proven in CSAPI or SensorML, and not new normative requirements. ## 6. What this means for the DIM manuscript and the original objective

**What is substantively supported:** The candidate DIM's five investigated distinctions are a *useful initial semantic foundation*. They prevent false classification of kind versus individual, allowed options versus actual configuration, method versus performed event, cabled constituent versus temporary deployment, and software representation versus hardware. The source/code check provides **narrow evidence** that one prototype rulebook can render selected known inputs under a shared page structure, while labeling important unknowns and keeping separate subjects separate.

**What is materially incomplete:** The original “whole System” Details objective needs more than these five distinctions. TRSS's estimator, OmniSense's direct voltage and six diagnostics, actual configuration/mode/operational source, communications, equipment state and managed lifecycle expose **additional domain meaning requiring its own sourced concept investigation**. A visually coherent single page or successful build **does not close this DIM coverage gap**. Conversely, a correct conceptual foundation alone is not enough: an actual producer must publish its data under valid selected semantics, and the interpreter must validate complete terms, source/subject context, values and provenance.

**What should not be concluded:** This study does **not** prove either that **CSAPI loses source data** or that a new standard extension is already required. The strongest static client weakness is **source-term namespace/type recognition**, followed by **under-interpreted diagnostic content and incomplete provenance presentation**. These are failures or limitations of the **current authored experiment's interpretation pathway**, not demonstrated flaws in OGC standards or vendor equipment. A source-to-UI loss analysis must ultimately distinguish: (a) vendor information never provided, (b) information lost/changed during a source→CSAPI transformation, and (c) correctly published information the client fails to recognize or display ([original problem baseline](../DIM_Profile_V2_Problem_and_Outcome_Baseline_v0.1_28OCT2026.md), §1).

**No changes or approvals:** No Step 10 text was silently rewritten, no new DIM name/root/classes/modules adopted, no Profile SHALL authored, no live Glaux deployment/code changed, and no new Smart Citizen experiment or source-hardware state was invented. The [Step 10 candidate manuscript](10_Candidate_DIM_Foundation.md) remains **CANDIDATE/NOT_ADOPTED**. The findings here are inputs to **Step 12's integrity/completion audit and bounded recommendation**, not an authorization to choose the next entire research roadmap.
