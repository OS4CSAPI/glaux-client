## Why this small study is worth doing

We now have source-qualified Integrated Sensor Architecture (ISA) and bounded, owner-accepted partial **The Open Group** Sensor Open Systems Architecture (SOSA) findings in one readable donor-to-Domain Information Model (DIM) master. Those donors describe different portions of whole sensing systems. Before we add the next donor, we should ask **how each original standard itself groups the information it defines**, what those groupings are meant to do, and what their organizing principles might teach us.

This is **not the same question** as grouping the 182 candidate bibliography artifacts by standards subject or source type, our five earlier candidate-concept research topics, our master crosswalk's existing topic sections, or the 329 comparison rows. Those project-created groupings are useful but cannot be attributed to donor authors. Existing [source-type/standardization stack](../DIM_Profile_V2_Source_Type_Assessment_and_Stack_v0.1_25OCT2026.md), [donor/concept comparison](../Concept_Donor_Comparison/README.md), and [ISA/SOSA synthesis](SYNTHESIS_ISA_SOSA_Lessons_for_Whole_System_DIM_and_CSAPI_Profile_12NOV2026.md) cover adjacent questions; none is a verified side-by-side inventory of the original donors' **native high-level organizational schemes**. Reuse their evidence, not their headings as presumed native donor facts.

**The practical question:** If the ultimate goal is one useful Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) API – Connected Systems (CSAPI) client Details view for unlike sensing arrangements, do original donor information groupings reveal durable distinctions, differing purposes, or omitted information that our own research organization obscures? A useful result would influence *later questions and testing*, not impose a final set of DIM classes, Profile sections, or UI tabs.

## Scope and evidence ceilings

**Initial comparison only:** the already-reviewed 2023 ISA original companion family and **The Open Group SOSA C239 Edition 1.1 (2023)**. Cite the exact original source, edition, clause/heading, grouping mechanism, and currently qualified depth. ISA's 480 Data Model Specification outcomes (474 supported; six definition questions open) and its original companion source records are documented in [ISA closeout](ISA22_Final_Validation_Closeout_09NOV2026.md). The Open Group SOSA original C239's **191/192 headings at least contacted** is *not* 191 fully audited sections; its [G4 original source ledger](SOSA_G4_F_192_Heading_Source_and_Master_Ledger_11NOV2026.json), [G4 review](SOSA_G4_F_Original_Corpus_and_Master_Reconciliation_11NOV2026.md), and [accepted G5-D decision](SOSA_G5_D_Owner_Disposition_Decision_Packet_11NOV2026.md) preserve **nine source-hold families**. The original eight C239 2023 native DSDM/IDL/OpenAPI companions remain unauthenticated/not fully audited; the authentic 2022 Edition 1.0 native model/IDL and the 2025 S251 material are **separate edition evidence, not verified 2023 substitutes**. **W3C SOSA/SSN is a different donor family**, never merge it with The Open Group SOSA.

Use the already-qualified OGC CSAPI Parts 1–2, SensorML 3.0 and SWE Common 3.0 findings **only as reference constraints** when interpreting what the future model/client must carry. Do not present them as a third fully audited native-grouping donor in this small comparison. Other donors, including SAPIENT, are **out of scope now**. Future qualified donor work may extend the same comparison artifact when approved and appropriate; do not launch an automatic 182-artifact survey.

**Distinguish types of grouping:** document chapters/headings; native model packages, entities, or data domains; architecture partitions and views; interface/API capability families; and operational/management categories. They can overlap or cut across one another. A table-of-contents heading does not itself establish a normative information category, a class hierarchy, an installed equipment assembly, or a client screen.

## Method: native meaning first, comparison second

1. Find the **actual original grouping statement or construct** in an examined source or its native artifact; distinguish author-declared organization from an analyst's convenient labels. Reuse stable source-item IDs, exact source locators and edition/access grades in the existing ISA/SOSA records. Where original material is only sampled, pointed to, not available, or unreviewed, say so explicitly rather than counting it as absent.
2. For each supported native grouping, record **what is grouped and why**, its grouping type, native term/definition, relationship to nearby groups, scope/exclusions, source locator and kind, Rule/Recommendation/Permission/Observation/definition/example/future status as appropriate, and whether the exact material was fully read, sampled, only referenced, inaccessible or unresolved. Use a compact table with source pointers, not an exhaustive duplicate of existing source ledgers.
3. Compare **organizational function and native semantics**, not label similarity or apparent donor vote counts. Classify an inter-donor relationship as supported overlap, narrower/broader, complementary/orthogonal, conflict, no established match or unreviewed. Examples and engineering-specific features stay qualified; a physical chassis organization is not automatically universal, and an ISA interface participant is not automatically a physical instrument.
4. Test *interpretive implications* against the existing cabled-probe, modular instrument, data-logger and Bennett's Point provenance examples **using qualified case evidence only**. Preserve design-versus-installed, observed-versus-derived and physical-versus-logical distinctions. Do not manufacture an equipment configuration or source-to-CSAPI transformation to make a category fit.
5. Treat organizational findings as **research evidence, not DIM or Profile adoption**. Do not invent new rows, classes, cardinalities, requirements, universal groupings, client tabs, or rankings. If a genuinely new, original-source-qualified row-level correspondence is warranted, update **the one existing canonical master JSON, both CSV mirrors, both Markdown comparisons, and the SAME editable Excel workbook** at that source-qualified checkpoint with independently checked cell parity and relevant original source coverage. If the study finds only organizational insights, leave all master formats unchanged and explicitly report **zero comparison/Excel lag**; never change the master just to copy a donor's headings.
