## 1. There is no single source-proven correct organization of every sensing system

The Integrated Sensor Architecture (ISA) and The Open Group Sensor Open Systems Architecture (SOSA) describe different aspects of sensing systems from different engineering perspectives. ISA emphasizes networked components, registration and communication, declared capabilities, information exchange and services. The Open Group SOSA describes physical system arrangements, logical modules, sensor-processing functions, implementation infrastructure, interfaces and management. Both perspectives may be relevant to the same equipment, without either being a comprehensive description of every vendor's system.

A weather station illustrates the difference. Physically, it may have instruments, a data logger, communications equipment, mounting and power supply. Functionally, it can measure, record, process and report. Operationally, its components may have configuration, health, mode, power and communications information. These are **different legitimate descriptions of one arrangement**, not mutually exclusive donor categories or automatic DIM classes.

**Accepted research lesson:** future DIM reconciliation should preserve distinct perspectives and their original meanings rather than copy one standard's table of contents or architectural taxonomy as an allegedly universal hierarchy. See [N2's 13 source-qualified seams below](#n23-purpose-by-purpose-source-comparison) and [the preceding ISA/SOSA plain-English synthesis](SYNTHESIS_ISA_SOSA_Lessons_for_Whole_System_DIM_and_CSAPI_Profile_12NOV2026.md).

## 2. Common information does not imply common physical equipment

ISA's 24 original capability bins organize declared possible functions and information, not 24 physical sensor types. The Open Group SOSA's functional modules also are not automatically separately purchasable or installed units; one physical product may implement several modules and different products may implement related roles. Their original purposes differ even when names appear familiar.

In the Campbell reference arrangement, a ClimaVue 50 may report multiple environmental quantities through a CR1000X logger. That does not establish a separate physical sensing system for every quantity, nor does the logger's reporting role erase the connected instrument's identity. Similarly, a vendor's network-visible Node or component identity is not automatically a physical serial number or full installed equipment inventory. A common DIM must not impose a SOSA card/chassis design on ordinary cabled instruments, or pretend an ISA network participant is necessarily a discrete physical sensor.

**Accepted research lesson:** equipment identity, a functional role, a communication participant and a digital representation can be related **without being identical**. Preserve installation-versus-design evidence and avoid inventing physical part-of relations from an interface or logical composition.

## 3. Whole-system descriptions are more than observations

Both source families substantively address concerns beyond the values being measured. ISA includes capabilities, configuration, status, services, events, commands, and participant/interaction information; SOSA separates system management from mission functions and addresses infrastructure, state, health, interfaces, operating modes and specialized power or software concerns. This supports **investigating** a whole-sensing-system information description rather than treating observations as the entire subject.

That support does **not** establish universal minimum information fields, optionality, status enumerations, exact DIM classes or any new CSAPI Profile SHALL. Source-specific Rules, permitted options, draft/future concepts and descriptive examples remain at their original scopes. ISA's six exact definition holds and SOSA's authentic 2023 native companion/edition/source holds remain open.

## 4. What this could mean for a common Glaux Details view

A useful, consistent client experience should eventually be able to help a user answer meaningful questions: **what is this individual sensing system; what real equipment contributes to it; how are the elements related; what can the system do and what does it report; what do we know about its current condition; which interactions are actually supported; and what evidence supports these claims?** These are **candidate user questions, not adopted UI tabs or DIM class names**.

A Campbell station could show connected meteorological equipment and a logger; a specialized modular SOSA system might show cards, functions and software run-time environments. The same client could present both coherently without claiming identical internal architecture, uniform data availability or verified device control. Unknown, inapplicable, source-example and derived information should remain distinguishable, and a command acknowledgement should not be presented as independent proof of physical effect.

A later design must still be tested against real source-qualified equipment data, existing CSAPI/SensorML/SWE capabilities, actual producer semantics and the project's explicit owner-controlled adoption gates. This document authorizes no Glaux UI, CSAPI endpoint or deployment change.

## 5. Two risks we should avoid

**Endless donor analysis:** there is diminishing value in repeatedly reexamining the same ISA and SOSA terms now that their bounded original-source reviews, canonical comparison and native-grouping analysis have documented the important distinctions. Reopening one should be prompted by a specific new original source, contradiction or owner decision, not another artificial AI plan.

**Premature universal taxonomy:** similarity between two standards is informative but does not prove that their categories are the right generic classes or required properties for every sensor. SAPIENT and later donors may add distinct subjects, capabilities, interface behaviors or relationship meanings that challenge the current conclusions. A missing established mapping is not proof of no equivalent anywhere in a donor lineage.

## N2.1 What the comparison actually says, in plain English

**Both donors describe capabilities, services, information exchange and system condition, but they do not partition the world in the same way.** ISA focuses heavily on participants, declared information and service interactions: how a component registers, presents capabilities, publishes values, receives requests and exchanges messages with a Controller. The Open Group SOSA describes an engineered sensor architecture using physical and service views, logical functional modules, implementation infrastructure, management arrangements, specialized interfaces and data-model layers. Neither perspective equates every physical constituent with an independently visible network participant or an independently produced observation.

The **strongest lesson is not a list of shared headings**. It is that a useful whole-sensing-system description may require several *distinct questions*: Which particular system and physical equipment are involved? Which functional and communication roles do they play? What can the system do, what was configured, what was observed, and what is known about current operating conditions? What makes each assertion credible? The questions are **candidate research concerns**, not adopted DIM sections, fields or UI tabs.

Importantly, there is **no verified architectural conflict** simply because SOSA has a hardware card taxonomy and ISA has a network-component taxonomy. Their subjects overlap differently. Nor does the apparent lack of a detailed ISA counterpart to a SOSA electrical profile prove the ISA corpus lacks all equipment concepts; the bounded record only supports **no direct grouping match established here**.

## N2.2 How to read the relationship judgments

These are **analyst comparisons of the purpose and meaning of original source groupings**, not donor-authored equivalences, accepted schema mappings, or unqualified cross-edition assertions.

- **RELATED / PARTIAL OVERLAP:** both address some of the same questions, but neither native group is a substitute for the other.
- **COMPLEMENTARY / ORTHOGONAL:** they address distinguishable parts, views or implementation layers of a larger concern; there is no implied exact class relationship.
- **NO ESTABLISHED DIRECT MATCH:** the assessed source scope does not justify the proposed direct correspondence; it is **not** a universal statement of absence.
- **UNREVIEWED / HELD DETAIL:** original native data, interface, rule or manufacturer evidence needed for a finer comparison is not authenticated or read to adequate depth.
- **CONFLICT:** reserved for a demonstrated incompatible source meaning at the same scope and standing; **N2 found no supported case of that kind** in these high-level organizational schemes.

When a row has multiple judgments, they apply to different parts of its statement, not one global "donor score." References `N1-I##` and `N1-S##` point to the **original inventory rows below**; each already links its edition, original clause and previously preserved source-item IDs.

## N2.5 Lessons and recommendations (for later decisions, not decisions now)

**First, we should model the questions, not photocopy a donor's chapter headings.** A universal hierarchy assembled by copying ISA's capability bins or SOSA's module list would falsely convert source-specific organization into generic equipment types. The groupings should serve as *checks* on whether an eventual DIM can answer meaningful questions across equipment families, not as an imposed set of classes.

**Second, physical, functional, communicative, informational and evidentiary descriptions are different perspectives.** One physical item may serve several functions or publish several values; a reporting component may relay another instrument's measurements; and an API resource may be a digital representation of either. A useful common client view could present known relationships coherently and expose source-specific details, **without requiring every system to populate identical internals**. That is a hypothesis to test, not an approved client layout.

**Third, status and management need subject qualification.** ISA participation stage, SOSA sensor/PIC/module states, current operating mode, measured power values, software environment and command disposition are not synonyms. The future cross-donor synthesis should verify both *who/what the statement concerns* and whether it is **declared, designed, observed, current, historical, derived or simply not known**. Do not replace unavailable information with invented defaults.

**Fourth, the meaning of "required" stays at its original scope.** ISA Controller service SHALLs, a SOSA physical interface Rule for a selected profile, a source Recommendation, a future module function and an application Profile SHALL are entirely different commitments. No donor "wins" by having more terms or source-packet crosswalk matches. The 2023 C239 native model/OpenAPI limits and six ISA source questions remain visible, not overcome by this comparison.

**Fifth, preserve heterogeneity deliberately.** A common client should eventually offer consistent meaning, provenance and handling of unknown/inapplicable information, while permitting specialized hardware cards, module run-time profiles, software arrangements or vendor-native information to remain specific. This study does not specify how to encode those distinctions in SensorML, SWE Common, CSAPI, or a UI component; those implementation choices remain owner-gated.

### Questions to carry into the next **authorized** donor, not answers invented in advance

1. What is the **next donor's original, source-native organizing principle**: a physical sensing platform, a system-of-systems architecture, a message/interaction model, an information model, or another subject?
2. Does it distinguish the **whole system**, constituent physical equipment, functional roles, communication nodes and data/reporting resources? Where does its own scope end?
3. How does it distinguish **declared capability, selected configuration, management condition, task request, reported outcome, actual observed effect** and original evidence?
4. What original **normative, recommendation, descriptive, draft, example, future or unreviewed** material supports each distinction, and which **edition/native companion files** must be obtained before saying it is comprehensively audited?
5. What would testing this organization against the **Dragino, AirGradient, Campbell and Bennett's Point** evidence falsify or leave unknown? Which differences ought to remain native rather than forced into a common shape?

These are **research questions only**. In particular, no SAPIENT taxonomy has been presumed or extracted here. The next donor should be studied source-first under its separately approved method and evidence-based closure gate, not rushed through an automatic copied N1/N2 schedule.

## Plain-language N1 result (within each donor only)

**Integrated Sensor Architecture (ISA):** Its original 2023 document family employs *multiple organizing schemes*: participating components/controllers and services; the lifecycle stages of their participation; network/presentation/message handling; catalogued services and capability bins; distinct properties/observables/commands; and the separate data types used to carry them. The source organization changes with the purpose of the companion document. The **24 capability bins** mix sensor/application modalities with general, electrical, networking and other concerns; they are **not a taxonomy of twenty-four physical sensor chassis types**.

**The Open Group Sensor Open Systems Architecture (SOSA), C239 Edition 1.1 (2023):** It likewise employs *several native views*: physical system interfaces, functional modules and information flows; logical modules versus implementing infrastructure; conceptual/logical/physical data descriptions; housekeeping/mission task distinctions; mission and support functions; mechanical/electrical hardware profiles; software run-time profiles; and inter-module interactions. Its native module taxonomy is **not an inventory of installed devices**, and parts of its documented module/interface ecosystem remain preliminary, reserved, future, or limited to specialized configurations. This document inventories those source-defined ways of grouping; it does **not yet compare ISA groups to SOSA groups**.
