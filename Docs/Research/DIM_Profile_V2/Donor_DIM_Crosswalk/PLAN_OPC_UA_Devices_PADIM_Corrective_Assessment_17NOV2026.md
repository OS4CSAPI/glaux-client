## Common assessment method

1. **Start from the donor.** Reconcile its own purpose, organization, sections, models, supporting definitions, figures and dependencies with the recorded inventory and actual contact depths. Existing findings must not become the boundary of discovery. Reuse adequate original-source examination; inspect originals for omissions, disputed meanings, unsupported generalizations and unanswered questions. A heading list, model enumeration or citation alone is not semantic coverage.
2. **Account for every current donor information cell.** Retain stable row IDs. Each cell receives an evidence-backed disposition: unchanged and justified, corrected, or explicitly unresolved with its reason. Group genuinely repeated meanings only with exact member IDs and exceptions. Check positive mappings and negative/unassessed statuses. Unreviewed never becomes absent or no-established through a blanket default.
3. **Compare in both directions.** Check existing rows against native meanings and native meanings against the whole current master. Record exact/direct, partial, broader, narrower, related, conflicting, no-established or unreviewed relations. Preserve subject, role, type/instance, declared/actual, supported/configured and command/effect distinctions. Generic labels such as parameter, capability or identity do not establish equivalence.
4. **Follow through on rejected or narrowed mappings.** Give the native meaning another adequate target, a justified new candidate, a reasoned native-only/out-of-scope disposition, or an exact unresolved question. Each proposed addition identifies the closest existing alternatives and what they fail to preserve. No additions quota, donor-exclusive-row target or presumption that a mature table must produce few additions.
5. **Classify independently of correspondence.** Apply the accepted F3 contract below with a source-based rationale. Check text, recorded classification, intended fill and actual saved fill separately. Blue is not shorthand for exact equivalence, universal applicability, implementation-specific status or adoption. Different colors for comparable meanings need an explicit semantic reason; first-donor privilege is not a reason.

Reuse adequate peer evidence from previously assessed donors where it tests the same meaning. Do not reopen those entire donors. A comparison must identify its native subject and scope, not merely matching labels or colors.
