## 2. Actual native meaning and source-qualified negative equivalences

**Legacy PICP means planned standard-profile transition, not a broken or old installed sensor.** The introductory source explicitly defines a *Legacy Plug-In Card Profile* (PICP) label as the consortium's **intention to remove** that hardware profile from a **future** edition, motivated by limiting physical profile combinations to maximize interchangeability. Merely finding "legacy" in Edition 1.1 is neither proof that a specified newer edition actually removed the profile nor a finding about actual installed equipment age, malfunction, End-of-Life, field hardware replacement, service life, or change to a CSAPI System identity. The source says the SOSA Consortium will determine removal timing, typically at a future release, but neither a removal decision/date nor the edition-specific contents of SOSA S251 Snapshot 3 has been checked here.

**Three different kinds of prescriptive status must remain distinct.** First, the four numbered, conditional SOSA Rules specify the physical slot and module profiles for an implementation **if it is a 3U Legacy Payload PIC or 6U Legacy Switch PIC**. Second, two numbered SOSA Recommendations say the listed legacy slot profiles **should not be used for new systems** and *anticipate* deprecation in the next edition; a **SHOULD** and a prediction are not a published SHALL that a fielded component was retired. Third, the unnumbered introductory narrative says the consortium determines a removal date; **after actual removal** a conditional **18-month grace period SHALL be provided** for certification submission, and submissions **after** that grace SHALL not proceed through certification against the removed legacy PICP. These two narrative SHALLs are source-authentic but **not separately numbered Rules**, and their applicability requires an independently established actual profile removal date. No current certification expiry or compliance result is inferable.

**Three specific subtype families, no blanket labeling.** §13.2.13.1 scopes the 3U Legacy Payload to slot **`SLT3-PAY-2F2U-14.2.3`** and **one of three** module profiles `MOD3-PAY-2F2U-16.2.3-{3,5,11}`. Source discussion notes many user-defined pins and resulting interoperability problems; this is a design/potential hardware interchangeability issue, not a source-proven installed external cable or observed network behavior. §13.2.13.2 scopes the 6U Legacy Switch to slot **`SLT6-SWH-16U20F-10.4.2`** and **one of four** printed module alternatives `MOD6-SWH-16F20U-12.4.2-{3,5,11,15}`. Their native label families differ (16U20F versus 16F20U); preserve the original identifiers without assuming typography or equality before reading actual ANSI/VITA originals. §13.2.13.3 identifies **ONLY** the **`-1` SMPM-10 dash option** of 3U Radial Clock `SLT3x-TIM-2S1U22S1U2U1H-14.9.2-n` as legacy and explicitly **excludes the remaining dash options**; it does not declare every radial clock PIC obsolete or rewrite previous §13.2.11.5.
