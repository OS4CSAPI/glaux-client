## 1. The problem, in the owner's terms

**The objective is not simply to integrate sensor systems into CSAPI and show their raw standardized descriptions. The objective is to enable a CSAPI client to present the important operational and equipment-specific information about different integrated sensing systems in a coherent, recognizable, and useful Details view—preserving as much of the meaning and organization of the source vendor's experience as is practical.**

The concrete original cases were **TRSS** and **OmniSense II**, two Unattended Ground Sensor (UGS) products from different defense contractors addressing closely related engineering and operational requirements. Both vendor environments communicate useful concepts such as sensor/detector or transducer selection, configuration, capabilities, power, communications, status and device operations. Yet they do not supply identical structures, names, value types, relationships, or workflows. A client built individually for either vendor can exploit its proprietary conventions; an arbitrary client consuming CSAPI/System descriptions cannot be assumed to understand those differences.

The owner observed that integrating source Systems through **OGC API – Connected Systems (CSAPI)** and **SensorML** and presenting them using only their generic conventions can yield a largely standardized *resource* view that is less operationally expressive than the vendor's native screens. The supplied **MASBUS** details screenshots illustrate a basic summary, extensive SensorML-derived lists, raw source representation and editor. The **MERLIN System Integrator** screenshots illustrate identity, procedure/configuration, capability/characteristic, subsystem, position, identifier and contact forms after integration. The early October 5 **Resource Details** PDF proposes one compact reusable frame with meaningful categories and disclosures. These are distinct things: **vendor-facing richness**, **standardized data availability**, and **human-usable interpretation**.

**Important qualification:** The hypothesis is *not* that CSAPI or SensorML inevitably destroy vendor data. Rich source meaning may survive a standards-compliant representation while a generic UI fails to recognize or organize it; alternatively, a particular source-to-CSAPI translation may omit important meaning. **Do not assert which loss happened without comparing the original source, actual produced representation and actual client rendering.** This distinction matters to the research question and its solution.

### Original user request and expected test

The owner started with two UGSs because the goal was to faithfully represent both in one **normalized but rich Resource Details UI/UX**. Their subtle differences showed why a two-vendor hard-coded screen would not solve the general integration problem. The question became: **what wider industry/community/government-standardized domain or recognized equipment framework legitimately contains the kinds of systems we expect to integrate?** Can its existing concepts organize diverse manufacturer descriptions, while a CSAPI Profile tells compatible clients what those concepts mean and where to find them?

**The owner does not want** one UI implementation for each proprietary vendor, a pixel-for-pixel clone of every manufacturer's interface, or an abstract research report displayed as a System resource. The aim is a **shared UI/UX whose categories and interpretation are consistent, but whose applicable contents, specialized details and qualified meanings remain faithful to each individual integrated System.**

This problem statement is a **project goal** rather than proof that the proposed design is already sufficient.

## 2. The proposed solution: chassis → DIM → Profile → client

The central **chassis** hypothesis remains sound as a *research and system-design approach*: first discover a defensible shared conceptual frame, informed by communities that already standardize related sensors and equipment, and then use that frame to organize the information that profile-aware integrations and clients exchange.

**Do not mistake the chassis for an already chosen superclass, final taxonomy or UI-tab list.** It is the question of the *scope and recurring concepts/relationships* capable of accommodating these systems, and the standards pedigree for reusing them. The current source-grounded working boundary is **equipment-realized Systems whose defining operational role is sensing/observing**, without excluding their communications, computation, command, power, lifecycle and non-sensing functions. Current CSAPI facets **systemType = Sensor** and **assetType = Equipment** describe different aspects. No final umbrella name, root class or complete module architecture has been adopted.

The intended division of labor is:

| Part | What it should do | What it must not be confused with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Donor and vendor investigation** | Find what the sources really describe; identify reusable community terms, distinctions, omissions, and relationships | Choosing new DIM classes solely because a source has a similar label |
| **Published Domain Information Model (DIM)** | Provide implementation-independent definitions, relationships, supported specializations, constraints and traceable meaning for our scoped sensing/equipment Systems | A fixed UI layout, a JSON encoding, the vendor's schema, or a copied CSAPI resource inventory |
| **Published CSAPI application Profile** | Select existing CSAPI/SensorML features and encoding/vocabulary conventions; add only independently justified, testable restrictions or obligations where necessary so implementations can publish predictable meaning | The DIM itself; a claim of all-donor conformance; unearned SHALLs |
| **Integration/mapping** | Map each vendor's actual evidence into honest, valid, profile-conforming CSAPI representations while retaining source provenance, units, identity scope, component relationships and meaningful differences | A license to invent missing data or to flatten everything into one pseudo-component |
| **Profile-aware CSAPI client prototype** | Recognize the Profile, interpret declared meanings and produce the shared Resource Details UI, showing specialized groups where appropriate and raw/unknown fields when necessary | One proprietary vendor-specific renderer per product, or a working end-to-end client merely because a local fixture was rendered |

**Intended information flow (proposal, not implemented service):**

Vendor information and verified System/source data → source-to-DIM semantic mapping → CSAPI/SensorML representation constrained by the eventual Profile → Profile-aware interpretation → common Resource Details frame with truthful, contextualized vendor detail and appropriate actions.

**Interoperability requires both sides:** The integrator must publish *sufficient, interpretable information*, and the client must implement the corresponding interpretations. Even a good DIM cannot recover source attributes an integration never captured, cannot guarantee manufacturer-like screen behavior without specified conventions, and cannot make unavailable hardware data true.

## 3. The shared UI goal—and the differences that must survive

The client should offer stable organization of useful System details, **not universal identical fields**. Likely recurring concerns across the initial examples include identity/kind, actual equipment and transducer/detector arrangements, capability, selected configuration, operational condition, communications, power, supported commands, provenance, source records, status and observations. **Those are areas of investigation and presentation, not prematurely adopted DIM modules.**

**An example of why real vendor differences matter:**

| Question the client must answer | TRSS historical fixture | OmniSense historical fixture | Semantic lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What sensing equipment/configuration is described? | Two specifically positioned detector slots and selected detector types | Reported transducer labels in a separate source case | Both concern sensing equipment, but a configured **slot** is not automatically one identified **transducer** |
| What is the power fact? | An estimated remaining-capacity percentage, based on estimator inputs | In another OmniSense source example, a 15.43 V DC reported voltage | Both belong in power/condition presentation, but **estimate ≠ measured voltage**; units and provenance must not be merged |
| What does a component mean? | Physical sensors/detector assemblies can be meaningfully discussed | Source records may label transducers | Source labels alone do not establish independent physical System identity or one universal part relationship |
| What does the client do with differences? | Show appropriate detector structure and its constraints | Show source-reported equipment, measured/estimated qualifiers, and unknowns | Shared navigation plus **honest, optional specialized detail** |

The Smart Citizen Kit 2.3 supplies an important third perspective: the **SHT31** produces temperature and humidity; one **AS7331** yields several UV channels; a cabled PM module normally sits inside the enclosure. Separately, the manufacturer's software calls a **Device–Sensor-definition database association** a **Component**. These distinctions prevent a naive generic client from inventing multiple independent physical sensors or mapping software associations to hardware subsystems.

**Unknown or unsupported details should remain visible as unknown or absent**, not be filled with guesses, silently discarded, or expressed as an identical empty template for every vendor. The client's available operations also depend on real command/control availability, role, scope and authorization, not on a generic Commands tab alone.

## 4. What we actually accomplished before and through 28 October

**A. Donor corpus, standards stack, chassis and candidate concepts.** A broad candidate donor population, source-type stack and subject/boundary analysis examined established industry, government and standards families. A working focus on equipment-realized sensing Systems emerged, and five candidate semantic distinctions were consolidated: individual System identity; reusable product kind; implementable Procedure/Method; composition/component standing; and digital representation/proxy identity. These **are not five newly adopted classes**. The earlier rushed **anatomy Phase B/C/D** promoted unjustified structure and remains **QUARANTINED** despite some older files calling it validated; later correction and research notes control the decision. The [five-pass synthesis checkpoint](DIM_Profile_V2_Five_Pass_Synthesis_Checkpoint_v0.1_26OCT2026.md) preserves the bounded findings.

**B. Completed Smart Citizen I01–I13 exercise, 27–28 October.** The team selected a well-documented commercial product and froze four original manufacturer/source repositories, 25 initial files, and 60 source-native facts (34 independently audited). The thirteen steps tested the above distinctions against manufacturer engineering documentation and code, produced a small **textual candidate** meaning/relationship slice, compared OGC accommodation, and critically challenged the mappings. The final [I13 case study](Case_Studies/Real_Equipment_DIM_Validation/I13_Integrated_Smart_Citizen_Case_Study_and_Gate_D_v0.1_28OCT2026.md) has **Gate D CONDITIONAL ACCEPTANCE of the research deliverable, NOT a successful empirical integration**.

Concrete earned value: separate product kind from an individual unit; configuration/method availability from executed measurements; sensing ICs from their output channels; physical parts from vendor software associations; permanent/temporal relationships from their appearances in links. I12 rejected four **incorrect hypothetical mapping shortcuts**, including populating observation-derived DataStream metadata from the product spec and mistaking parent-associated child streams for direct parent production. **No actual SCK2.3 physical individual, live Observation, unique ID join, operational sensor inventory or lossless CSAPI transformation was verified**. Research completeness and prototype validity cannot be inferred from 13 completed steps.

**C. The first Smart Citizen wireframe was NOT the requested test.** The initial separate page [Smart Citizen DIM research walk-through](https://glaux-client.pages.dev/?page=resource-details-smart-citizen) filled the Resource Details frame mostly with research explanation and limitations rather than a source-based System details UI. The owner explicitly said this was **not** the intent. It remains preserved only as optional historical/contextual material, not the correct prototype direction.

**D. The corrected cross-vendor comparison IS on the intended path.** On 28 October, the separate [Profile-aware Resource Details semantic test](https://glaux-client.pages.dev/?page=resource-details-profile-test) was deployed on Cloudflare Pages. It uses the **same 560 × 440 Resource Details template**, selectable source cases, a **generic CSAPI-style field listing** versus a **candidate profile-aware renderer**, and a small set of **term-based** rules that maps source fields into meaningful shared categories while preserving source-specific groups/qualifiers. It displays **SCK2.3 manufacturer product-design information** and the **unchanged synthetic SensorML-shaped TRSS ETU-II, OmniSense Core2 and Core5ADS fixtures**. The owner assessed it as **janky but on the right path, at an appropriate primitive stage**, because actual information from JSON is beginning to populate the canvases and reveal what works and fails. This is **a direction accepted for investigation, not approval of final UX, class taxonomy or Profile**.

**Crucial limitation:** The current experiment **does not yet retrieve actual CSAPI System instances and apply an adopted Profile to server responses**. The TRSS and OmniSense fixtures are synthetic and carefully labeled; Smart Citizen facts are manufacturer product/design descriptions written into the prototype, **not a retrieved individual System JSON**. The generic-vs-profile-aware comparison illustrates the hypothesis; it does not prove all generic CSAPI clients behave identically or any standards defect. The rulebook is a *temporary client interpretation*, not a machine-readable published Profile or complete DIM.

## 5. How research will become the two published specifications and useful UI

The end products are **a publishable, implementation-independent DIM** and **a publishable CSAPI application Profile**, supported and tested by a **DIM/Profile-aware CSAPI client prototype**. Research reports, evidence registers and UI wireframes are **supporting materials**, not substitutes for those products. The existing [Provisional Document Outlines](DIM_Profile_V2_Provisional_Document_Outlines_v0.1_26OCT2026.md) distinguish the intended publications without pretending they are already populated.

**Working loop, not a newly mandated multi-iteration plan:** examine one concrete cross-vendor information need (for example, selected detector slots versus reported transducer lists); state its original vendor meaning and source evidence; compare donor-native concepts; propose a **qualified candidate DIM meaning/relationship**; determine exactly what existing CSAPI/SensorML structures could carry it and what, if anything, a Profile would need to constrain; render the **same sourced information** in the shared UI and check whether meaning survives; then decide what warrants inclusion in evolving publication candidates. Keep **unknown/unresolved** with their evidence and return them for further research.

**Each meaningful finding should be traceable along one intelligible chain:**

Original vendor fact / reference → donor semantic basis → candidate DIM concept and relation → proposed Profile/encoding interpretation → client presentation / permitted action → source-fidelity result and limitation.

The owner should be able to look at such a chain and see **how a research result becomes a better System Details experience** and eventually a definition or requirement in a publication. **Do not force every research note directly into a normative specification; require evidence and review.** When a section is mature enough, draft it in the provisional published-DIM format with an explicit **candidate/accepted/open** standing; derive Profile rules later where the actual interoperability use case warrants them. Formal test and conformance assertions require real, specified obligations and qualifying representations. Avoid more research-only iterations disconnected from a visible model/UI problem.

## 6. What a stronger future proof would need

This is **a proposed evidence ladder, not an approved task list**:

- **Structural plausibility:** Recognized, donor-grounded concepts actually cover the important source differences in more than one vendor without special-casing the vendor name. Unknowns and specializations remain visible. **Current synthetic comparison starts here.**
- **Representation fidelity:** A documented integration crosswalk maps original TRSS and OmniSense meanings into valid **actual CSAPI/SensorML representations** subject to a testable draft Profile; the client reads those JSON resources through the CSAPI client path, not by embedding fields directly in TypeScript.
- **Comparative client fidelity:** Compare vendor source screens, encoded resources and profile-aware UI results, marking **preserved**, **qualified**, **omitted**, **unavailable**, and **misrepresented** fields and relationships; verify overlapping categories and vendor-specific differences.
- **Executable/conformance evidence:** When the DIM and Profile have justified rules, assess observable client/server behavior, constraints, optionality, versioning, source provenance and applicable conformance tests. Real devices/live observations may be needed for specific operational claims, but should not be fabricated merely to finish an early visualization prototype.

An accepted proof **does not mean** duplicating every proprietary UI feature, accepting every vendor's semantics unchanged, requiring identical values across device families, or guaranteeing all broader systems fit automatically. The success measure is **useful shared presentation while preserving genuine differences and clearly declaring what was lost or could not be sourced**.

## 7. Authority and non-drift rules

1. **This project goal controls direction.** Before substantial research, model decisions, or UI development, ask how that work will improve faithful cross-vendor CSAPI System Details representation and the future DIM/Profile publications. If the answer is unclear, **explain the proposed value to the owner before inventing an iteration**.
2. **No final chassis/domain name/class anatomy has been authorized.** Use source terms, donor-native meanings and exact edition/conformance qualifiers. The current equipment-realized sensing System boundary is *working*, not a universally agreed taxonomy. V1's eight information subjects are prior requirements evidence, not automatic V2 modules.
3. **Quarantine remains:** earlier over-promoted Anatomy Phase B/C/D decisions are not accepted model authority even when old documents label them validated. The newer concept/pedigree work may be read as research, with its own evidence limits.
4. **Never confuse standpoints:** manufacturer source design ≠ fielded physical unit; product kind ≠ individual System; algorithm description ≠ executed method; source Component database association ≠ physical part; field/measurement channel ≠ independent System; reported voltage ≠ estimated percentage; a theoretical OGC carrier ≠ completed binding; simulated fixture ≠ validated CSAPI server response.
5. **Client UI is an independent proof layer, not the DIM.** UI categories should be validated against real source distinctions, but an attractive page cannot justify a domain class or normative requirement. Conversely, nominal OGC feature support without usable source-to-UI mapping does **not** prove the user's problem was solved.
6. **Do not automatically continue the old case-study plan.** I01–I13 are **closed**; PITON C's suggestion to trigger a post-case-study research decision checkpoint from a bare "proceed" was **an AI-invented next step explicitly challenged by the owner** and is **superseded** by the new [PITON v1.5](PITON_DIM_Profile_V2_Continuity_v1.5_28OCT2026.md). The owner has not authorized another multi-step plan or an immediate choice between "more Smart Citizen data" and "capability/performance". Their current request is documentation and a durable PITON.
7. **Communication:** normal, concise, understandable explanations first, then targeted research as necessary. At the end of each authorized iteration explain what changed in our understanding/actual DIM/Profile/client, what it enables, what's still not proved, and **one** clearly identified owner-approved continuation. Do not hide behind long reports or assume the owner can infer the value of many iterations from their completion.
8. **Document standing:** Original sources and normative standards control factual claims; research reports supply reasoned synthesis; the approved user statements in this document control **the problem and intended outcome**. Future corrections must be recorded explicitly. Do not make undocumented changes to deployed wireframes or research branches.
