## 1. What we learned in ordinary language

### ISA: a component's network appearance is not necessarily its physical identity

The **Integrated Sensor Architecture** explicitly distinguishes an **ISA Component**, which participates in a software/interface interoperability architecture, from an **ISA Composition**, whose member equipment is exposed through a **Composition Interface Component (CIC)**. A CIC can make a member *visible* and announce its capabilities, make it *opaque* while exposing its capabilities under the CIC's own identity, or expose a non-ISA member as a **virtual ISA Component**. A visible member may be **transparent** (externally taskable) or **translucent** (visible but not externally taskable). These are **source-defined choices about representation and interaction**, not proof that a real sensing element exists or ceases to exist depending on whether the network announces it. Original source: **ISA Software Design Document 6.0r5, §4.2.2 and Fig. 2, printed pp. 10–12**.

The same ISA model distinguishes a Component Capability Description (CCD) into **Properties** (reportable information about the Component's state), **Observables** (information it can publish about observations) and **Commands** (actions it can be requested to perform), together with **declarations** and actual messages that carry values or outcomes. This gives us a useful **description-versus-value-versus-action** structure; it is not the same as a particular SensorML Procedure or CSAPI DataStream. Primary: **ISA SDD 6.0r5 §5.1.1 (printed pp. 15–16); ISA Data Model Specification 6.0r12 §2.0 Capability Bins (printed pp. 3–8)**.

A particularly useful source caveat: **ISA SDD §3.0 calls ISA a software-only solution relying on physical host networks, computing and timing infrastructure**. The ISA Component is therefore a standards-defined *software-interface participant*, not a reliable synonym for a physical circuit board or CSAPI System. ISA source terms and version-specific data-model vocabulary are still valuable; a source's primary *interface* classification does not diminish their conceptual importance.

### The Open Group SOSA: the whole Sensor, its realization, and its management can be described separately

The Open Group SOSA **Technical Standard, Edition 1.1** treats the **SOSA Sensor** as the overall bounded sensing subject. It may comprise **one or more hardware elements**, including software/firmware, with hardware grouped or distributed **on or within a host platform**; the **Host Platform** is a *separate* physical context rather than another generic sensor component. SOSA modules encapsulate functions and interfaces, while **infrastructure** provides hardware/runtime/interconnect support. **System Manager** manages configuration, health, control and security of the Sensor/components; **Task Manager** coordinates sensing mission tasking. Those are **two different functions and responsibilities**, not two physical boxes or an all-purpose Procedure class. Original: **Edition 1.1, C239, §§4.1.1.1–4.1.1.3, 4.2 Table 4.2-1, 5.1, 6–6.1**, especially printed pp. 21–24 and 37–39.

The independent **Edition 1.0 SOSA Domain-Specific Data Model (DSDM)** is even more explicit about *relationships*: it defines `Sensor` (a whole-system Entity), `Component_` (a physical **or software** functional entity), `Module_` (a physical **or software** unit with defined interfaces), `SensorComponent` (a part-of association), `HostPlatform` (a separate Sensor–Platform association), `Product` and `ProductInstance` (manufactured product associated with a **functional** Component), and `ModuleImplementation` (Module–Component realization). It further distinguishes whole-sensor health, operational/configuration state, faults and executed built-in-test results. **Exact DSDM relationship participants and cardinalities belong to the Edition 1.0 native model; they are NOT imposed on V2 or silently relabeled Edition 1.1 requirements.** Original: **SOSA DSDM (April 2022), §§1.1.3.15, .54, .68–69, .82–83, .89–92**, printed pp. 13, 30, 36–37, 42, 44–46.

**Why both donors matter:** ISA shows that **visibility/identity in a network is mediated and configurable**, while SOSA shows that **the physical whole, its functional components, manufactured implementations, its host and system-management activities need not be collapsed into one component tree**. Both make our previous physical-whole-versus-interface distinction more precise. They do not prove one universal vendor-independent chassis class. Our initial five findings remain credible but are **not yet sufficiently developed** to capture these dimensions in a full DIM.

## 6. What the original equipment examples show, without inventing data

**TRSS detector selections.** ISA's **visible/opaque/virtual membership** warns against converting every source detector slot to an externally registered independent System. The Open Group SOSA's **Slot/Chassis/Hardware Element** structure is more concrete for physical assembly, but TRSS slot configuration is not thereby proven to be SOSA-conformant hardware, a PIC, or a fixed installed unit. Distinguish a **reported selected detector option**, its physical place and its external interface representation.

**OmniSense Core2/Core5ADS.** Source-reported Core2 transducer labels are not five ISA Components, five SOSA functional Modules or five CSAPI UIDs. Separate Core5ADS voltage and six per-test diagnostic findings offer useful future **status/health/fault/BIT** questions; neither ISA nor SOSA licenses the client to synthesize one overall “healthy” claim or equate volts with battery percentage.

**Smart Citizen Kit 2.3.** Its **Data/Urban Boards and in-enclosure cabled PM module** illustrate a physical assembly; the software backend's `Component` is **Device–Sensor-definition data**, not SOSA `Component_` or ISA Composition member. ISA's ability to hide/virtualize members and SOSA's separation of physical Component from function/ProductInstance reinforce the warning: **a source's “component” label alone cannot determine physical part, measurement channel, product type or CSAPI System standing**.

**Practical client payoff:** A future common Details view could distinguish **(1) physical whole and actual constituents, (2) functional roles and source capabilities, (3) source representation/visibility, (4) manufacturer product/implementation, (5) platform/host and mission/task context, (6) state, test and management provenance**. This is *possible user-facing information organization*, not an adopted screen/tab design, standardized model class list or proof of source-to-CSAPI fidelity.
